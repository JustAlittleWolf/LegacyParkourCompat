# Dry oak-fence side cue for falling water changes in 1.12

In 1.11.2 Ornithe Feather, `LiquidBlock.isFaceSolid` rejects same-material neighbors, accepts UP, rejects ICE, then delegates to `Block.isFaceSolid`. The horizontal face of a dry ordinary oak fence qualifies through its solid `Material.WOOD`. In 1.12 Ornithe Feather, the ordinary branch requires `FaceShape.SOLID`, and `FenceBlock.getFaceShape` returns `FaceShape.MIDDLE_POLE` for a horizontal face. The same dry oak-fence neighbor no longer qualifies. [Exact boundary note](../../source-evidence/20261117/block-a-falling-water-oak-fence-1112-112/falling-water-wall-face-solidity-boundary.md) · [Independent verdict](../../source-evidence/20261117/block-a-falling-water-oak-fence-1112-112/verdict.md)

In both releases, `LiquidBlock.getFlow` tests the horizontal neighbor and the cell above it at `LEVEL >= 8`. When the loaded water scan admits the central cell, with zero horizontal depth gradient and no other qualifying side, the fence cue yields flow `(0,-1,0)` in 1.11.2 and zero flow in 1.12. On the ordinary local-player route, that isolated nonzero contribution adds `-0.014` to vertical velocity after the aggregate is normalized. The evidence establishes this conditional current write. It leaves full scan equivalence, other fence variants and runtime trajectories open.
