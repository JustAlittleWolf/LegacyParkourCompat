The complete `EntityFluidInteraction.java` is identical in the inspected 26.1.1, 26.1.2 and 26.2 published-unobfuscated sources; their client-cache `getChunk` has the same missing-chunk return at lines 69–77.

Difference: the older body update calls `touchingUnloadedChunk`, but its four-coordinate `LevelReader.hasChunksAt` calls virtual `hasChunk` on the logical client level. `ClientLevel.hasChunk` unconditionally returns true. The inflated box therefore never rejects a client scan for missing chunks. It is not a physical cache-availability test on this side. Fluid reads delegate through `Level.getFluidState`, `getChunkAt`, the three-argument `getChunk` and the four-argument overload with `true` to `ClientChunkCache`; an absent client chunk returns `emptyChunk`, whose fluid state is EMPTY. This fallback does not request or generate chunks. Loaded cells still contribute water/lava contact, height and current. Eye sampling runs afterward, independently of the old body guard.

The newer shared scan first resets height, eyes and accumulated current, then checks every chunk in the interaction box's integer scan range padded by one horizontal block. `getChunk(FULL, false)` returns null for any absent client chunk; the helper immediately rejects, even if another checked chunk contains fluid. Both body and eye results remain dry, and no fluid current is accumulated. Its additional section `hasFluid` optimization does not change this missing-neighbor witness.

Movement meaning: take a non-riding, non-flying player beyond the first tick at X=15.4, Z=8, feet Y=64, with the normal approximately 0.6-wide box. Chunk (0,0) is loaded and contains water at the player's body and eye column, including Y=64 through 66; chunk (1,0) is unavailable. The deflated body scan has X block 15 only, whereas the newer preflight pads that to X=14 through 16 and queries unavailable chunk (1,0). The older client admits the body scan and records positive WATER height, `wasTouchingWater=true`, and wet eyes. The newer helper rejects and leaves height zero, body WATER false and eyes false. For an ordinary fluid-affected player this changes the `shouldTravelInFluid` input and selects water travel versus air travel; a nonzero sampled flow can additionally produce an old current impulse while the newer scan produces none. Replacing water by lava similarly changes positive LAVA height/contact after the first tick. These are source-derived consequences, not runtime parity measurements.

The minimum operation established here is historical logical-client fluid preflight admission. Reinstating an inflated-box physical chunk-cache requirement would reproduce neither the old virtual dispatch nor this witness. Restoring shared scan admission can recover both wet body and wet eyes in this case, so missing chunks do not establish a separately necessary eye-eligibility operation. Historical eye sampling still requires its own strict fluid-top comparison, previous-water snapshot and boat-specific mask; those semantic differences must not be erased by using a body WATER mask for eyes. Whether another case requires an independently resolved eye operation is not established by this loading difference. Applicability before 1.21.11 is unproven here; the inspected endpoint establishes the change by 26.1 without identifying an intermediate snapshot boundary.
