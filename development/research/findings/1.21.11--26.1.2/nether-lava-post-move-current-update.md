# Post-move lava-current refresh

1.21.11, Mojmap: `net/minecraft/world/entity/Entity.java`, `move` (748–752); `net/minecraft/world/entity/LivingEntity.java`, `checkFallDamage` (350–353); `net/minecraft/world/entity/Entity.java`, `updateInWaterStateAndDoWaterCurrentPushing` (1509–1522).
26.1.2, unobfuscated: `net/minecraft/world/entity/Entity.java`, `move` (767–771), `updateFluidInteraction` (1558–1581), and `getFluidInteractionBox` (4015–4023); `net/minecraft/world/entity/LivingEntity.java`, `checkFallDamage` (344–347); `net/minecraft/world/entity/EntityFluidInteraction.java`, `update` and `Tracker.applyCurrentTo` (32–89, 171–188).
Difference: After `Entity.move` updates position, the local-authority path calls `checkFallDamage`. In 1.21.11, `LivingEntity.checkFallDamage` refreshes fluid movement by calling `updateInWaterStateAndDoWaterCurrentPushing`, which scans/applies water current only. In 26.1.2 it calls `updateFluidInteraction`; that update scans tracked fluids and, when fluid pushing is enabled, applies both water and lava currents. `checkFallDamage` gates this refresh on `!isInWater()`, whose water flag is cached by the preceding base-tick fluid update. Consequently a player whose cached water flag is false (including a player in lava) reaches the post-move refresh in both versions, but only 26.1.2 applies lava current at this point. The 26.1.2 lava operation still requires `isPushedByFluid()` and uses the dimension-selected lava scale.
Movement meaning: A player moving through Nether lava can receive an additional lava-current delta-movement contribution after the move has already changed position and before subsequent travel processing. This timing contribution is absent from the 1.21.11 post-move refresh, which only applies water current. The contribution follows the normal fluid scan and cutoff rules; it is not a second independent cutoff/depth rule.
