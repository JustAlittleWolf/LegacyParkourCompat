Difference: Entity enumeration changes in 1.14.3. Through 1.14.2 it queries the scan bounds expanded by 0.25 and filters collision boxes against the original bounds. From 1.14.3 it queries and filters against bounds expanded by 1.0E-7, with an average-side-length empty check. Actual client dispatch reaches these providers: 1.13.2 `World.getCollisions` appends entity collisions; 1.14.4 `WorldView.getCollisions` invokes the `WorldAccess` override forwarding to `EntityView`. Neither `ClientWorld` replaces those methods. Both streams place block obstacles before entity obstacles.

Movement meaning: The source-level query difference alone does not establish a changed auto-jump decision. Local players inherit `Entity.getCollisionAgainstShape` returning null. The only non-null vanilla obstacle providers found are boats and living shulkers, both returning their entity bounding box; the hypothesized collision box extending beyond entity bounds is therefore unavailable here. For player heights above 0.51F, the two later obstacle-test segment bounds lie inside the original scan bounds. Any obstacle passing that test must intersect the original scan bounds and is admitted by both entity queries. Epsilon-only extra shapes fail the later test. The positive player scan dimensions also make the new empty check immaterial in these poses.

A concrete reachable witness remains required before this can establish an independent movement difference. Sleeping/dying dimensions of 0.2F do not satisfy the containment argument, but require independently establishing nonzero auto-jump input, the on-ground gate, the head-space checks and a consequent next-tick jump. Ordinary sleeping and death screens release all key bindings (`Minecraft.openScreen`, 1.14.4 lines 725–751). Changed block stream ordering, border handling and block collision providers are distinct dependencies and cannot substitute for an entity-query witness.
