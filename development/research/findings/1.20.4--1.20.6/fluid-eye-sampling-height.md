# Player eye-fluid sampling height

1.20.4, Mojmap: `net/minecraft/world/entity/Entity.java`, `tick`, lines 436–439 and `updateFluidOnEyes`, lines 1211–1223; `net/minecraft/world/entity/player/Player.java`, `tick` and `updateIsUnderwater`, lines 250–251 and 314–317; `net/minecraft/client/player/LocalPlayer.java`, `aiStep`, lines 693–710, and `isUnderWater`/`updateIsUnderwater`, lines 1059–1080.
1.20.5, Mojmap: `net/minecraft/world/entity/Entity.java`, `tick`, lines 438–441 and `updateFluidOnEyes`, lines 1227–1239; `net/minecraft/world/entity/player/Player.java`, `tick` and `updateIsUnderwater`, lines 270–271 and 334–337; `net/minecraft/client/player/LocalPlayer.java`, `aiStep`, lines 697–714, and `isUnderWater`/`updateIsUnderwater`, lines 1066–1087.
1.20.6, Mojmap: `net/minecraft/world/entity/Entity.java`, `tick`, lines 438–441 and `updateFluidOnEyes`, lines 1227–1239; `net/minecraft/world/entity/player/Player.java`, `tick` and `updateIsUnderwater`, lines 270–271 and 334–337; `net/minecraft/client/player/LocalPlayer.java`, `aiStep`, lines 697–714, and `isUnderWater`/`updateIsUnderwater`, lines 1066–1087.

Difference: `Entity.updateFluidOnEyes` first copies the old `isEyeInFluid(WATER)` result into `wasEyeInWater`, clears `fluidOnEyes`, chooses a sample Y, then checks boat overlap, selects the block position, and adds fluid tags only when the fluid surface is strictly greater than the sample Y. The sample is `getEyeY() - 0.11111111F` in 1.20.4 and `getEyeY()` in 1.20.5 and 1.20.6; the remaining ordering and strict comparison are unchanged. In a non-boat case with unchanged eye height and water at that position, a water surface above the lowered 1.20.4 sample but at or below eye height is tagged at the 1.20.4 resample and not at the 1.20.5/1.20.6 resample.

Player path and timing: `Player.tick` calls `updateIsUnderwater()` before `super.tick()`. That method sets `wasUnderwater` from the current `isEyeInFluid(WATER)` tag. For `LocalPlayer`, `isUnderWater()` reads this `wasUnderwater` latch; it does not use `Entity.isUnderWater()` (which is `wasEyeInWater && isInWater()`). The entity tick later calls `updateFluidOnEyes`, producing the eye-fluid tag used by the next player-tick latch. Thus a changed sample affects local-player underwater movement gates on the following tick, after the tag has been resampled. In `LocalPlayer.aiStep`, `isUnderWater()` participates in sprint start/stop conditions, including underwater start impulse handling and the condition `isInWater() && !isUnderWater()` that cancels sprinting. This gives a reachable player movement effect at the waterline; it does not establish an immediate same-tick change to the local-player latch. Entity-level swimming also reads `Entity.isUnderWater()`, but is a distinct consumer from the local-player override and should not be substituted for this player-tick chain.

Movement meaning: For a local player with the eye in water but with the surface in the interval between the two sample heights, 1.20.4 can retain/set `wasUnderwater` from its lowered sample where 1.20.5/1.20.6 cannot. On the subsequent player tick this can alter the underwater sprint gates described above. The exact introduction patch is not established between 1.20.4 and 1.20.5.

Applicability checkpoints: the same lowered eye-fluid threshold is present at 1.16.5 (`Entity.updateFluidOnEyes`, line 984), 1.17.1 (line 1093), 1.18.2, 1.19.4 and 1.20.1; 1.20.4 retains it. These inspected endpoints support the lowered-sample behavior through 1.20.4. The 1.20.5 and 1.20.6 exact sources use eye height. For 1.13.2, Ornithe Feather `Entity.isSubmergedIn`, lines 1042–1050, uses the threshold `eyeY < fluidSurface + 0.11111111F`; this source pins the version as 1.13.2 / Ornithe Feather, not Mojmap. For 1.14.4 and 1.15.2, Mojmap `Entity.isUnderLiquid`, lines 1057–1069 and 1040–1052 respectively, uses the equivalent threshold. These releases use a different sampling implementation. Do not project the 1.20.4 `updateFluidOnEyes` operation or its boat/block-position side effects back to those versions. Applicability is therefore split: the equivalent threshold is observed at these older checkpoints; the paired `updateFluidOnEyes` sample change is established at the adjacent stable releases 1.20.4 and 1.20.5, with 1.20.6 confirming the newer behavior. No stable release intervenes in this pair. The exact snapshot where the change was first introduced is unknown, and does not leave the stable-profile cutover open.
