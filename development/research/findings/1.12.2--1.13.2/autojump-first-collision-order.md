Both versions keep the first collision box intersecting either side segment, save its `maxY`, and break. Without Jump Boost, a hit schedules auto-jump only if the resulting height over the player's feet is `> 0.5` and `<= 1.2`; the following player tick consumes timer 1 by setting `input.jumping=true` (1.13 timer consumer: `LocalClientPlayerEntity.java`, lines 707–711). The change is the order of boxes exposed to that first-hit loop. In 1.12.2, `World.getCollisions` visits candidate block positions x,z,y and returns the collision boxes in that order. In 1.13, `WorldView.getBlockCollisions` aggregates and optimizes block shapes; `autoJump` iterates `getBoundingBoxes()`, whose `VoxelSet.forEachBox` emits the decomposed boxes in x,y,z order. Thus a lower-y component from a later z cell can precede an earlier-z, higher-y component.

**Witness state and realistic input/effect producer.** At autoJump entry use a vanilla player of width `0.6`, height `1.8`, yaw `0°` (+Z), at `(x,feetY,z)=(0.88,0.5,1.075)`, supported by a bottom slab at `(1,0,1)`. Set forward input to `1`, sideways input to `0`, sprinting true, Speed III (amplifier 2), full hunger, no Jump Boost, auto-jump enabled, not sneaking or riding, and timer zero. Speed's operation-2 modifier is `0.2 × (2+1)=0.6`; sprint adds operation-2 `0.3`; base movement speed is `0.1`, so the resulting speed is approximately `f=0.208`. `LivingEntity` damps forward input by `0.98F`, so a grounded forward move requests about `0.204` blocks. The near fence clips the player from center z=`0.928` at center z≈`1.075`, where the player's forward AABB face reaches that fence's z=`1.375` face. `LocalClientPlayerEntity.move` calls `autoJump` after `super.move` even when collision clipped the move. At autoJump entry the actual move delta is about `dz=0.147`, so `vec3d2` is already `postMove + (dx,dz)`; the endpoint is `postMove + (dx,dz) + unitDirection*p`, not just `postMove + unitDirection*p`. Since `p=max(7f,1/l)≈1.456`, its z coordinate is about `1.075+0.147+1.456=2.678`, past the lower post's near face at z=`2.375`. This is ordinary forward+sprint input with an ordinary Speed effect state. These coordinates and movement are source-derived; no runtime trajectory was executed.

Place unconnected fences at `(0,1,1)` and `(0,0,2)`. All other blocks in the auto-jump clearance locations are air, including `(0,2,1)`, `(0,3,1)`, `(0,1,2)`, and `(0,2,2)`; there are no other colliders or entities in the query. The support slab's top equals the player's feet, so it does not intersect either ray. The fence posts share x extent `[0.375,0.625]`; at entry, the side-ray x coordinates are `0.58` and `1.18`, and both rays are at y=`1.01`. Only the x=`0.58` ray intersects the posts; the x=`1.18` ray misses them. The first ray's endpoint is z≈`2.678`, so it crosses both posts, and both fences are inside the grown collision query. The source tests either side ray, so this one intersecting ray suffices.

In 1.12.2, the x,z,y block query encounters the high fence at `(0,1,1)` before the low fence at `(0,0,2)`. Its post has `maxY=2.5`, giving `u=2.0`; the empty block above it leaves that height unchanged, so no timer is scheduled. In 1.13, the union's lower fence box is emitted before the upper fence box: their x extents match, and at the lower fence's y=0 slice the upper fence is absent. The lower post has `maxY=1.5`, giving `u=1.0`; the empty block above it leaves the height unchanged, and timer 1 is scheduled. Fence collision shapes in 1.13 are the central post from y=0 through 24/16, as `PaneBlock` passes the fence's 24/16 height into the shape returned by `getCollisionShape`.
