1.13, Ornithe Feather: `net/minecraft/world/WorldView.java`, `getBlockCollisions`, lines 113–151, retains the asymmetric upper-Y-only pruning. 1.13.1, the same class/member at lines 112–151, introduces two-axis-face pruning; its complete source file is byte-identical to 1.13.2.
Difference: Both collision scans use the padded swept player bounds. The 1.12.2 scan still inspects cells at an X side face and the minimum-Y face when Z is interior; the 1.13.2 predicate skips a cell whenever it is on query-region faces for any two axes. Fence collision shapes extend to Y=1.5 and an east-connected arm reaches X=1. A piston-translated fence can therefore extend across both X and Y faces into a cell visited by the older scan and skipped by the newer scan. For example, with the fence at `(0,0,z)`, moved east by `0.5`, an eastward arm spans X=1.125–1.5 and Y=0–1.5. A player whose box starts at minX=1.6 and minY=1.1 and moves left by 0.2 has a swept query that reaches X=1.4; the cell is on the query’s minimum-X and minimum-Y faces while Z is interior. The older provider includes the fence shape; the newer provider filters out that cell.
Movement meaning: In that reachable piston-and-fence setup, the older collision resolver can clip the player’s leftward movement against the translated arm while the newer block scan can let the movement proceed through it. This is a direct player response to a piston.
