# Forced rotation clamps pitch before wrapping
1.21.8, Mojmap: `net/minecraft/client/multiplayer/ClientPacketListener.java`, `handleRotatePlayer` (801–808); `net/minecraft/world/entity/Entity.java`, `setXRot` (3709–3714); `net/minecraft/server/level/ServerPlayer.java`, `forceSetRotation` (1065–1068); `net/minecraft/server/commands/RotateCommand.java`, coordinate overload of `rotate` (77–82); `net/minecraft/commands/arguments/coordinates/WorldCoordinates.java`, `getRotation` (27–30).
1.21.10, Mojmap: `net/minecraft/client/multiplayer/ClientPacketListener.java`, `handleRotatePlayer` (796–806); `net/minecraft/world/entity/PositionMoveRotation.java`, `calculateAbsolute` (38–60); `net/minecraft/world/entity/Entity.java`, `setXRot` (3781–3786); `net/minecraft/world/entity/LivingEntity.java`, `updateFallFlyingMovement` (2404–2426).
Difference: With the same decoded absolute rotation (both newer relative flags false), 1.21.8 passes packet pitch directly to `setXRot`, which wraps with `% 360.0F` before clamping to ±90. 1.21.10 first clamps pitch to ±90 in `calculateAbsolute`, then passes it to the same setter. A finite packet pitch of 360 therefore produces pitch 0 in 1.21.8 and 90 in 1.21.10. The old `/rotate` coordinate path does not normalize its `WorldCoordinates.getRotation` result before `ServerPlayer.forceSetRotation` sends it, so multi-turn pitch is a concrete source-reachable input.
Movement meaning: The direct client rotation response differs for multi-turn absolute pitch. For a dry, fall-flying player who is not on a climbable block, the next travel tick reads pitch and the look vector in `updateFallFlyingMovement`, changing its gravity/lift and horizontal steering terms. This concerns the response to an already decoded rotation; the added relative-rotation protocol capability and server command production are not the emulated operation.
