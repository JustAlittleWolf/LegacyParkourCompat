Difference: 1.8.9 starts the X/Z candidate loops at `floor(shape.minX/minZ)` and ends them at `floor(shape.maxX/maxZ + 1.0)`; its Y loop starts at `floor(minY) - 1`. From 1.9, all three axes use `floor(min) - 1` through (exclusive) `ceil(max) + 1`. The new code omits the extreme X/Z corner columns and their top/bottom Y cells, then dispatches each retained state through `BlockState.addCollisions`. The 1.9, 1.9.1 and 1.9.2 methods match; the 1.9.3 and 1.9.4 methods match. The player movement path calls `World.getCollisions` from `Entity.move`; in 1.9+, moving-piston collision shapes also translate the moved state's shape by the piston progress and union the previous and current positions, so a candidate block outside the player box's ordinary cell range can contribute an intersecting collision box.
Movement meaning: For a fixed 1.8.9 profile, a player near a moving piston can receive a different collision list when its shape reaches across a block-cell boundary. The extra candidate-cell scan can include that box in collision clipping and alter the resolved player displacement. This is a source-derived movement consequence; runtime parity was not tested.
