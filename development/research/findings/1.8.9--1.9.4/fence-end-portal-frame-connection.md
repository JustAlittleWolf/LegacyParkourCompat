Difference: The frame has `Material.STONE` and reports non-solid rendering in both versions. In 1.8.9 it nevertheless inherits `Block.isCube() == true`; 1.9.4 overrides `isCube(state)` to return false. The fence's generic neighbor branch requires solid-blocking material and a cube, excluding pumpkin. The frame is neither a fence nor a fence gate, and stone is solid blocking and not pumpkin. Thus oak, spruce, birch, jungle, dark oak, acacia and nether-brick fences connect to a neighboring frame in 1.8.9 and do not in 1.9.4. Each registry entry uses `FenceBlock`; the six wooden variants use wood and nether brick uses stone, but the same-material fence special case does not apply to a frame. All four frame facings and both eye values have the same connection result. Eye only adds the frame's own central collision box above its 13/16-high base.

Movement meaning: Collision recomputes connections from the current vanilla neighbor state. With a fence at `(0,0,0)`, a frame at `(1,0,0)` and air at its other three horizontal neighbors, 1.8.9 supplies the east-connected box `[0.375,1] × [0,1.5] × [0.375,0.625]`; 1.9.4 supplies only the central post `[0.375,0.625] × [0,1.5] × [0.375,0.625]`. An airborne ordinary player with `minX = 0.7`, `maxZ = 0.2`, normal width/height, feet at `y = 1.1`, and displacement `(0,-0.02,0.3)` overlaps the old arm's X/Y ranges after the Y move while clearing the post and the frame itself, including an eye. With no other obstacles, the old arm clips Z displacement to `0.375 - 0.2`; the newer provider permits `0.3`.
