# Nether portal contact requires intersecting the portal slab

1.21.4, Mojmap. `1.21.4/mojmap/net/minecraft/world/level/block/NetherPortalBlock.java` 47–69 defines the portal outline but inherits the full-cell `Shapes.block()` inside-contact default from `world/level/block/state/BlockBehaviour.java` 353–355. `world/entity/Entity.java` 1050–1084 bypasses the swept-shape test for that sentinel.

1.21.5, Mojmap. `1.21.5/mojmap/net/minecraft/world/level/block/NetherPortalBlock.java` 49, 61–69 adds `getEntityInsideCollisionShape`, returning the axis-dependent portal outline. `world/level/block/Block.java` 168–175 and `world/phys/shapes/Shapes.java` 344–350 establish its central 4/16-wide slab. `world/entity/Entity.java` 1079–1120 and 1129–1133 now require a swept intersection with that shape unless it is the full-cell sentinel. Portal registration remains `noCollission()` (`Blocks.java` 2024–2030 in 1.21.4; 2084–2090 in 1.21.5).

A standing player can occupy the portal cell with its center beside the slab: with width 0.6 and center offset 0.05 from the nonslab side, the player AABB reaches only 0.35 while the slab starts at 0.375. Short or stationary movement still visits the cell. The portal callback therefore runs in 1.21.4 and is skipped in 1.21.5 at this position.

Movement meaning: Keep a normally opened survival `InventoryScreen` active while contact is recorded, with the physical jump key held. An eligible living local player must be awake, not a passenger, without portal cooldown, and affected by blocks; normal block scanning excludes removed or `noPhysics` entities (`Entity.isAffectedByBlocks`, 1.21.4 `Entity.java` 781–782; 1.21.5 817–818). The inventory screen is non-pausing and closes during the portal transition before the next `input.tick`. The mouse was released when the inventory opened; on a non-macOS system with the game window active, `MouseHandler.grabMouse` repolls key mappings only when mouse capture is currently ungrabbed. A still-physically-held jump key is then sampled before the grounded jump gate; with jump available (on ground and no jump delay), `LivingEntity.aiStep` can call `jumpFromGround` and raise Y velocity. Opening the inventory sends a container-close packet; the inspected server handler only closes the menu.

Evidence: 1.21.4 `Minecraft.java` 1840–1845, `InventoryScreen.java` 18, `AbstractContainerScreen.java` 666–668, `LocalPlayer.java` 642–663 and 844–866, `MouseHandler.java` 359–364, `KeyMapping.java` 55–58, `KeyboardInput.java` 22–31, `LivingEntity.java` 2739–2753; 1.21.5 `Minecraft.java` 1861–1866, `AbstractContainerScreen.java` 666–668, `LocalPlayer.java` 682–703 and 870–892, `MouseHandler.java` 398–403, `KeyboardInput.java` 22–31, `LivingEntity.java` 2725–2739. The result is a reachable local input/movement effect, not a claim of server movement acceptance.
