Difference: the older source refreshes eyes separately after body WATER/LAVA scanning. It snapshots previous eye WATER, clears current tags, samples `BlockPos.containing(getX(), getEyeY(), getZ())`, and records the sampled fluid's tags only when block Y plus fluid height is strictly greater than eye Y. `getEyeY` is position Y plus eye height without subtraction. A non-underwater boat suppresses all eye tags only when its bounding box contains eye Y inclusively; its body WATER suppression instead applies at every eye height and does not suppress body LAVA. Underwater boats suppress neither operation.

The older body guard calls `LevelReader.hasChunksAt`, which dispatches to `ClientLevel.hasChunk`; that override always returns true. Thus missing neighboring client chunks do not make the older body guard reject scanning. Loaded cells can supply body height/current and eye membership while missing cells read as empty. The newer shared operation resets all trackers before its real `getChunk(FULL, false)` availability check, and any missing checked chunk leaves both body and eyes dry. The eye result is additionally restricted to scanned blocks on the eye X/Z column and the fluid's inclusive bottom/top interval. Non-underwater boats clip or null the shared interaction box for every tracked tag.

Movement meaning: a non-riding crouching player at X=15.4, Z=8 can have feet and eyes in loaded chunk 0 water while chunk 1 is missing. The older client body scan proceeds and its eye point is wet. The newer padded scan reaches X=16 and rejects both results. `Player.tick` caches the previous eye WATER result before `super.tick`, and `LocalPlayer.isUnderWater` returns that cache without a body conjunction. On a following tick with forward+sprint input, sufficient food, no item use/restriction/collision and initially not swimming or sprinting, the older cached wet result permits the crouch exception to sprint start; old body WATER plus cached underwater makes shallow-water false. The newer dry cached result denies the crouch exception. This source-inferred witness demonstrates the shared preflight change; it does not isolate an eye-only effect from the simultaneous body difference.

Refreshing the older eye point and preserving its previous-water snapshot are source requirements, including the strict comparator and distinct boat eye gate. The client missing-chunk witness does not prove that eye refresh needs a separately resolved eligibility operation: permitting the historically available body scan through a shared scanner can also recover the wet eye result. A repair must distinguish the old body WATER boat mask from its all-tag, eye-height-specific mask; applying the body WATER mask to eye membership would change source behavior. Whether a separate eye operation is necessary after restoring shared body availability requires an independently closed witness. This evidence establishes no sample-coordinate applicability before 1.21.11.
