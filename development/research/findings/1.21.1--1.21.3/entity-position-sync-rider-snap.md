# Entity position sync immediately repositions a local player riding a vehicle

Scope: direct local-player position and old-position writes from `ServerEntity` position synchronization while the player is a vehicle passenger, limited to the immediate-correction branch. Vehicle physics, other packet response paths and full pair coverage are outside this draft.

1.21.1, Mojmap: `net/minecraft/client/multiplayer/ClientPacketListener.java`, `handleTeleportEntity`, lines 535–549; `net/minecraft/world/entity/vehicle/Boat.java`, `lerpTo`, lines 224–231; `net/minecraft/server/level/ServerEntity.java`, position sync selection around lines 142–151; `net/minecraft/world/entity/Entity.java`, `rideTick`, lines 1920–1925.
1.21.2, Mojmap: `net/minecraft/client/multiplayer/ClientPacketListener.java`, newly added `handleEntityPositionSync`, lines 555–578; `net/minecraft/world/entity/vehicle/AbstractBoat.java`, `lerpTo`, lines 208–215; `net/minecraft/server/level/ServerEntity.java`, entity position sync selection, lines 147–153; `net/minecraft/network/protocol/game/VecDeltaCodec.java`, fixed-point encode and component deltas, lines 7–17 and 31–41; `net/minecraft/world/entity/Entity.java`, `positionRider`, lines 2014–2023 and `setOldPosAndRot`, lines 1472–1475.
1.21.3, Mojmap: the relevant `ClientPacketListener.java`, `ServerEntity.java`, and `Entity.java` sources are byte-identical to 1.21.2; the corresponding members and ranges are unchanged.

Difference: 1.21.1 `ServerEntity` sends `ClientboundTeleportEntityPacket` when the tracked position codec overflows, teleport delay exceeds 400, riding state changes, or on-ground state changes. `ClientPacketListener.handleTeleportEntity` passes `3` to `entity.lerpTo`, but the 1.21.1 `Boat.lerpTo` override ignores that argument and sets `lerpSteps` to `10`. The boat therefore interpolates for ten steps; its rider follows in `Entity.rideTick` through `vehicle.positionRider(this)`. Starting in 1.21.2, `ServerEntity` sends `ClientboundEntityPositionSyncPacket` under those sync conditions. The new client handler lerps only when the entity is ticking and its squared distance from the packet position is at most `4096`. Otherwise it immediately `moveTo`s the vehicle to the packet position and rotation; if `hasIndirectPassenger(localPlayer)` is true, it then calls `positionRider(localPlayer)` and `localPlayer.setOldPosAndRot()`. The base `Entity.positionRider` writes the passenger position using `Entity::setPos`. This creates an immediate local-player position and old-position update on the exceptional correction path rather than following the boat's ten-step interpolation. The exact cutover is 1.21.2: the packet class and handler are absent in 1.21.1, present in 1.21.2, and the relevant 1.21.2 and 1.21.3 source files are byte-identical.

Witness dependencies: 1.21.1 `net/minecraft/client/multiplayer/ClientPacketListener.java`, `handleSetEntityPassengersPacket`, lines 978–1005; `net/minecraft/world/entity/vehicle/Boat.java`, `canAddPassenger` and `getControllingPassenger`, lines 858–869. Boat admission requires fewer than two passengers and `!isEyeInFluid(FluidTags.WATER)`, so the witness boat must not have its eyes submerged when the riders board. In 1.21.2 and 1.21.3, the corresponding listener method is lines 1031–1061 and the boat methods are in `net/minecraft/world/entity/vehicle/AbstractBoat.java`, lines 796–807; its passenger-admission method retains the same eye-in-water gate. `net/minecraft/network/protocol/game/ClientboundSetPassengersPacket.java`, constructor, lines 17–24, copies passenger IDs in the vehicle's passenger-list order. The control and passenger tests are `net/minecraft/world/entity/Entity.java`, `hasIndirectPassenger` and `isControlledByLocalInstance` (1.21.1 lines 2986–2997; 1.21.2/1.21.3 lines 3136–3151).

Movement meaning: a reachable witness is a two-player boat with a remote player first in passenger order and the local player second; the boat is not eye-submerged when they board. The boat allows two passengers and selects its first living passenger as controller. The passenger packet preserves the vehicle's passenger-list order and the client starts riders in that order. Thus the local player is a passenger (accepted by `hasIndirectPassenger`, which also covers direct passengers) while `isControlledByLocalInstance` is false because the boat's controlling passenger is remote. Keep the server and client sync gates distinct: `ServerEntity` sends on codec overflow (a signed-short encoded component outside [-32768, 32767], approximately an 8-block delta from its codec baseline), or independently on teleport delay, riding-state or on-ground changes. The client snaps only if the vehicle is not ticking or its current position is more than 64 blocks (squared distance strictly greater than 4096) from the packet position. For a large server teleport, both distance conditions hold when the server codec baseline is still aligned with the client's old boat position; alternatively, a non-overflow server trigger can send the packet while the client distance gate is met. The sync handler then directly places the vehicle and local rider. The passenger-control predicates and witness are confirmed in exact source; the concrete position consequence follows from the called `positionRider` writer.
