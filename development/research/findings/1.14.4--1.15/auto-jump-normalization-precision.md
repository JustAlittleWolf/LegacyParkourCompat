# Auto-jump normalization changes from double to float approximation

1.14.4, Mojmap: `decompiled_minecraft/1.14.4/mojmap/net/minecraft/client/player/LocalPlayer.java`, `move`, lines 845–849; `updateAutoJump`, lines 856–949. `util/Mth.java`, `fastInvSqrt(double)`, lines 395–400. `world/phys/Vec3.java`, constructor lines 15–18, `cross` / `add` lines 38–55, `scale` / `multiply` lines 79–92 and `lengthSqr` lines 99–100.

1.15, Mojmap: `decompiled_minecraft/1.15/mojmap/net/minecraft/client/player/LocalPlayer.java`, `move`, lines 851–855; `updateAutoJump`, lines 862–953; `canAutoJump`, lines 962–969. `util/Mth.java`, `fastInvSqrt(float)` / `fastInvSqrt(double)`, lines 394–407. `world/phys/Vec3.java`, `cross` / `add` lines 43–60, `scale` / `multiply` lines 84–97 and `lengthSqr` lines 104–105.

Difference: The normalization input remains a float on both sides. Actual horizontal movement is computed as double position differences, cast separately to float, and passed to `updateAutoJump(float,float)`. Its direction vector stores those arguments as doubles and computes `x*x + y*y + z*z` in double arithmetic; the result is cast to float before the `<= 0.001F` gate. The small-movement fallback also remains float speed times float input, float sine/cosine, float multiply/add/subtract expressions, followed by a double-vector squared length cast to float.

In 1.14.4, `float n = (float)Mth.fastInvSqrt(i)` promotes that float argument to the only available double overload. It computes `half = 0.5 * input`, reads `Double.doubleToRawLongBits(input)`, applies `6910469410427058090L - (bits >> 1)`, converts back to double, then returns `seed * (1.5 - half * seed * seed)` with one Newton correction in double arithmetic. Only the result is narrowed to float.

In 1.15, `float n = Mth.fastInvSqrt(i)` selects the newly present float overload. It computes `half = 0.5F * input`, reads `Float.floatToIntBits(input)`, applies `1597463007 - (bits >> 1)`, converts back to float, then returns `seed * (1.5F - half * seed * seed)` with one Newton correction in float arithmetic. Preserve the left-associated multiplications and their float rounding; computing the old double approximation and casting once does not reproduce this operation.

Movement meaning: The resulting float is promoted to double by `Vec3.scale(double)` to scale the horizontal direction. It also controls the float expression `Math.max(speed * 7.0F, 1.0F / n)`, the probe endpoint, the width-offset cross product, the inflated search box and the two endpoint-pair bounding-box intersection predicates. Thus the changed approximation can alter obstacle candidates or the `-0.15F` forward-direction gate near a boundary, and consequently whether auto-jump is scheduled. This is a source-derived consequence; no gameplay parity result is asserted. The endpoint-pair predicate is an axis-aligned bounding-box intersection, not an exact segment raycast: `world/phys/AABB.java`, `intersects` overloads, lines 204–216, are identical in these two versions. Successful height checks write `autoJumpTime = 1`; the next local AI step decrements it and sets `input.jumping = true` (1.14.4 lines 638–641; 1.15 lines 644–647).

The caller and helper files match within each of those two patch pairs.

Exact 1.19 Mojmap has `client/player/LocalPlayer.java` `updateAutoJump` float input/call at lines 984–1004 and `util/Mth.java` `fastInvSqrt(float)` at lines 485–491; 1.19.1 and 1.19.2 have those ranges at lines 990–1010 and 486–492, below their exact `decompiled_minecraft/<version>/mojmap/net/minecraft/` roots. The stable precision boundary is therefore 1.14.4 to 1.15. The later 1.19.4 switch to `Mth.invSqrt(float)` is a separate operation. Snapshots were not examined.

Native applicability starts with the inspected 1.10 source. Exact 1.9 and 1.9.4, Feather gen2 `+build.2`, have no local auto-jump field, move override, normalization caller or obstacle-probe scheduler in `client/entity/living/player/LocalClientPlayerEntity.java` (complete classes, lines 1–781 and 1–787 respectively). Their local AI steps at lines 600–750 and 604–755 do not inject an auto-jump input; searches of the inherited client/player/living/entity movement hierarchy likewise find no such path. Although their math helper already exists, these two releases have no native auto-jump operation to normalize. This finding does not introduce auto-jump behavior for those profiles; other pre-1.10 releases were not examined here.

This finding isolates normalization precision. The 1.15 refactoring of eligibility into `canAutoJump`, its block jump-factor gate, and position/base-height expressions are not claimed to be equivalent by this precision comparison.
