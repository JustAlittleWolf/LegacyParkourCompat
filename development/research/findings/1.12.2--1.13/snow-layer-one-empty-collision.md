# Snow layer-one empty collision shape

1.12.2, Ornithe Feather: `1.12.2/ornithe-feather/net/minecraft/block/SnowLayerBlock.java`, `SHAPES` and `getCollisionShape`, lines 25–35 and 66–71. The `LAYERS=1` collision result is a `Box` with local bounds `(0,0,0)-(1,0,1)`: `i = LAYERS - 1` is zero, so the returned maxY is zero while minY is zero.
1.13, Ornithe Feather: `1.13/ornithe-feather/net/minecraft/block/SnowLayerBlock.java`, `SHAPES` and `getCollisionShape`, lines 25–35 and 68–77. `LAYERS=1` returns `SHAPES[0]`, which is `VoxelShapes.empty()`; subsequent entries are positive-height voxel shapes.
Difference: the old zero-height AABB is retained by the old collision collector when the expanded movement query straddles its plane (strict `Box.intersects`), then old Y-axis clipping treats its maxY as a landing surface. The 1.13 empty voxel shape contributes no collision interval. A reachable fixed-state witness is one layer of snow (LAYERS=1) placed directly atop an eight-layer snow block: both versions permit the upper snow because `canBePlaced` explicitly accepts same-block support at LAYERS=8 (1.12.2 lines 84–92; 1.13 lines 83–91). In old collision geometry the lower eight-layer snow ends at 7/8 block height and the upper layer's degenerate plane is at its block bottom, so a player descending onto the upper cell lands 1/8 block higher than the lower snow's collision top. In 1.13 the upper layer is empty and the player descends to the lower snow's 7/8-height collision top. This establishes a local vertical movement difference for the fixed arrangement, not complete tick-trajectory parity.
