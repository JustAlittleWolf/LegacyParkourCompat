Difference: In 1.13.2 the cauldron collision shape is the unit cube minus the inside box `[2,14] × [4,16] × [2,14]` in sixteenths. In 1.14 it additionally subtracts three bottom boxes: `[0,16] × [0,3] × [4,12]`, `[4,12] × [0,3] × [0,16]`, and `[2,14] × [0,3] × [2,14]`. The union helper is `VoxelShapes.m_19519917`, which reduces with OR (1.14 `VoxelShapes.java`, lines 112–117). The top cavity and its floor at 4/16 stay unchanged. The shape is independent of cauldron water level and entity collision context. Cauldron does not override the inherited collision method; collision is enabled by its ordinary block registration.

Movement meaning: A standing ordinary player can jump below a floating cauldron. For example put a full support block at (0,61,0), a cauldron at (0,64,0), and center the standing player at x=z=0.5 with feet at y=62. The approximately 0.6-wide box fits entirely in the new central bottom cavity (x/z 2/16 through 14/16), while the approximately 1.8-high head starts below y=64. Its first normal jump displacement exceeds the approximately 0.2 old headroom. In 1.13.2 upward clipping stops the head at y=64; in 1.14 and 1.14.4 it stops at y=64+3/16. Thus the changed shape permits an additional 3/16 block of upward displacement for a normal player; this is a concrete ceiling-clipping difference, not merely raw component geometry. This inference assumes loaded ordinary blocks, no other obstruction/entity/world-border collision, no no-clip, no cobweb, and vertical jumping with no horizontal step candidate. It is derived statically, without runtime parity execution.

Boundary: the changed geometry is present in exact 1.14, and remains present in exact 1.14.1 (Yarn build.10), 1.14.2 (Yarn build.7), 1.14.3 (Yarn build.13), and 1.14.4 (Ornithe Feather gen2 build.2). Each intermediate CauldronBlock keeps the same four-box subtraction and inherits `Block#getCollisionShape` delegating to its outline shape when collidable. The release cutover from the older endpoint is therefore 1.14.
