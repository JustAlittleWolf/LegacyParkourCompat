Difference: 1.8.9 stores six mutable bounds on the registered AnvilBlock singleton. Anvil.updateShape overwrites those bounds from the supplied position's FACING, but inherited collision collection reads the existing bounds without updating from the colliding position. In 1.9 collision collection delegates through the queried BlockState to getShape, which chooses SHAPE_X or SHAPE_Z directly from that state's FACING on every query. The 1.9 form persists in inspected 1.9.4.
Movement meaning: a previous shape query for an X-axis anvil can make a Z-axis anvil collide as X-axis in 1.8.9. This is reachable through ordinary player targeting, not merely inferred from a render helper: Minecraft.tick line 1314 calls GameRenderer.pick(1F), pick lines 318-325 invokes Entity.rayTrace lines 1029-1033, World.rayTrace lines 648-652 / 745-749 reaches Block.rayTrace, and that calls the anvil's updateShape. Minecraft then ticks entities at line 1635. With no intervening anvil shape writer, subsequent player movement uses those shared bounds. This is a conditional prior-query-state bug; an unconditional assertion that every 1.8.9 anvil always uses the wrong axis is false.
