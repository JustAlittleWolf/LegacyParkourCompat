## Compared paths and results

In `aiStep()` (1.19.3 line 652; 1.19.4 line 645), sprint eligibility/`sprintTime` and auto-jump normalization changes are excluded by assignment. Remaining jumpable-mount selection changes from `getVehicle()` + `canJump(this)` (line 542) to `getControlledVehicle()` + `canJump()` (line 535). `rideTick()` changes the boat-input callback guard from `getVehicle() instanceof Boat` (line 877) to `getControlledVehicle() instanceof Boat` (line 858).
- `net/minecraft/world/entity/PlayerRideableJumping.java`, `AbstractHorse.java`, `Camel.java`, and `Entity.java`: `getControlledVehicle()` is the vehicle whose controlling passenger is this player (Entity line 2821). The existing horse implementation has a one-passenger admission gate and reports its first passenger as controller; its old and new `canJump` methods both reduce to `isSaddled()`. The Camel's 1.19.3 `canJump(Player)` also requires that player to equal `getControllingPassenger()`; the 1.19.4 no-argument predicate retains its movement gate, while LocalPlayer's controlled-vehicle check enforces the same controller identity. Thus the jump-input path and its `START_RIDING_JUMP` packet consumer do not establish a changed gate for the source-present rideable jumpers. `ServerGamePacketListenerImpl` correspondingly changes its `START_RIDING_JUMP`/`STOP_RIDING_JUMP` lookup from `getVehicle()` to `getControlledVehicle()` and calls the new predicate; no additional client/server discrepancy was found.
- The boat callback change can stop a non-controlling second boat passenger from writing directional input into the boat's input state, and can leave that player's local `handsBusy` false for those directions. `Boat` is a non-player vehicle and its physics are excluded; no direct local-player movement or pose difference is established by this callback.
- The `moveTowardsClosestSpace()` BlockPos change in LocalPlayer (line 414→413) replaces `new BlockPos(double,...)` with `BlockPos.containing(...)`. The former delegates to `Vec3i(double,...)` which floors each coordinate; the latter calls the same `Mth.floor` operations. No state/velocity difference.
- The confusion effect timer predicate in LocalPlayer (`getDuration() > 60` → `!endsWithin(60)`) is a portal progression gate, not a movement predicate. No movement change established.
- `LocalPlayer.isEffectiveAi()` remains `true` (1.19.3 line 493; 1.19.4 line 486). Although `LivingEntity.travel()` drops its `isEffectiveAi() ||` term, the 1.19.4 `Entity.isControlledByLocalInstance()` falls back to `isEffectiveAi()` when there is no controlling passenger. LocalPlayer has no controlling passenger of its own, so this gate remains true for local-player travel. No new ground/air travel-admission difference follows for LocalPlayer.
- `Entity.setPose()` remains `entityData.set(DATA_POSE, pose)` in both versions; `LocalPlayer`'s standing-pose reset, `Player.tick()` spectator/on-ground state writes, and the `Input`/`KeyboardInput` sources are unchanged. No other scoped pose or ground/air gate change was found.
- Direct correction protocol: 1.19.3 `ClientboundPlayerPositionPacket` carries `dismountVehicle`, and `ClientPacketListener.handleMovePlayer()` unlinks before applying the packet's relative/absolute coordinates and rotations. 1.19.4 removes that flag/handler step. `Entity.removeVehicle()` updates passenger membership; `ServerEntity.sendChanges()` now emits the passenger-list packet and then an absolute `connection.teleport(...)` for a removed `ServerPlayer`. The client passenger-list handler is otherwise textually unchanged; it ejects/reassigns passengers before the position correction is processed. The packet protocol/order changed, but the examined writers and consumers still supply detach plus the server-selected absolute player position/rotation. No changed movement value or final player-state delta is established. Intermediate state between these distinct packet callbacks is a remaining ordering question; this comparison does not assert an intervening movement tick or runtime result.
