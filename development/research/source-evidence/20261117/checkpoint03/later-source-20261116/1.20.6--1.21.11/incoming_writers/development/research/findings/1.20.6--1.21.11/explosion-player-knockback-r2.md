Difference: 1.20.6 casts a present knockback vector's components to floats when constructing the packet, transports three floats, then unconditionally adds the returned components to the local player's velocity after `Explosion.finalizeExplosion(true)`. An absent vector is stored as three `0.0F` components and follows the same unconditional addition. 1.21.11 transports an optional `Vec3` through `Vec3.STREAM_CODEC`; its components are doubles, and the listener calls `Entity.addDeltaMovement` only when the optional is present. For a present finite nonzero impulse whose components are not exactly representable as floats, packet transport can therefore produce different additions and next-tick movement state.
Movement meaning: absent impulse is equivalent for ordinary finite nonzero existing velocity components. It is not bitwise equivalent when an existing component is `-0.0`: the old listener adds `+0.0` to it, and IEEE double addition produces `+0.0`; the new empty optional skips the addition and preserves `-0.0`. This is a finite, exact movement-state difference. No immediate displacement or later trajectory consequence is claimed.

The changed packet/listener form is bounded by the inspected adjacent releases 1.21.1 → 1.21.2: 1.21.1 retains the three-float payload and unconditional addition; 1.21.2 uses `Optional<Vec3>` and conditional addition. See `decompiled_minecraft/1.21.1/mojmap/net/minecraft/network/protocol/game/ClientboundExplodePacket.java` and `ClientPacketListener.java`, and the corresponding 1.21.2 files (record/codec lines 15–27; `handleExplosion` lines 1225–1241; `net/minecraft/world/phys/Vec3.java`, component addition, lines 116–117). This establishes that adjacent boundary only; it does not claim complete movement-pair coverage or inspect every intermediate patch.
