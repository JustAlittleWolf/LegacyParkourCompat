# Lava travel and held-jump consumers
1.16.1, Mojmap: `net/minecraft/world/entity/LivingEntity.java`, `travel(Vec3)` 1912–1976 (lava branch 1957–1976), `jumpInLiquid` 1900–1902, held-jump block 2424–2451; `Player.isAffectedByFluids` 1005–1007; `LocalPlayer.tick` 183–186.
1.16.2, Mojmap: `net/minecraft/world/entity/LivingEntity.java`, `travel(Vec3)` 1914–1978 (lava branch 1959–1978), `jumpInLiquid` 1902–1904, held-jump block 2426–2453; corresponding `Player`/`LocalPlayer` methods have the same behavior apart from line offsets.
Difference: The compared `travel(Vec3)`, held-jump block, and `jumpInLiquid(Tag<Fluid>)` bodies are byte-for-byte equal after LF normalization. Their `isInLava()` and lava-depth inputs changed with the Entity producer/predicate. The unchanged travel lava branch requires effective AI or local control, `isInLava()`, `isAffectedByFluids()`, and inability to stand on the fluid at block position; for the local player, effective AI is true and affected-by-fluids is false only while flying. After entry it applies relative input at `0.02F`, moves, then at depth `<= getFluidJumpThreshold()` scales velocity by `(0.5, 0.8F, 0.5)` and applies fluid-falling adjustment; at greater depth it scales all components by `0.5`. It then applies `-d / 4.0` vertical gravity unless no-gravity, with a horizontal-collision escape that may set Y to `0.3F`.
Held jump runs under `jumping && isAffectedByFluids`. It selects lava depth when `isInLava()` is true and water depth otherwise, then uses the unchanged ground/depth branches. `jumpInLiquid(LAVA)` and `jumpInLiquid(WATER)` have the same exact body: add `0.04F` to Y. Therefore, in a water-and-lava state, selecting the other tag does not by itself establish a different vertical impulse. The 1.16.1 water-first cache update can leave lava depth zero while the callback flag is true; 1.16.2 still refreshes lava depth after water. Held-jump consequences must account for both `isInWater()` and selected depth.
Timing and witness: On the ordinary local-player tick path, `Entity.baseTick` clears `firstTick` before `LivingEntity.aiStep` handles held jump and travel, so the new first-tick guard is not a later-consumer gate. Under the producer finding's conditional loaded, lava-only near-surface witness, with the player airborne, held jump set, fluid effects enabled, no water, and after the base-tick refresh, old code selects `jumpInLiquid(LAVA)` and adds `0.04F` Y; new code sees no positive cached depth, does not select a fluid-jump impulse, and skips the lava travel branch. This establishes an operation-level source consequence, not an exact trajectory result. The witness remains conditional on `FluidTags.LAVA` membership and was not runtime tested.
Coverage: No independent travel/jump body change was found. This consumer finding depends on the producer/predicate finding; keep their evidence and acceptance separate.
