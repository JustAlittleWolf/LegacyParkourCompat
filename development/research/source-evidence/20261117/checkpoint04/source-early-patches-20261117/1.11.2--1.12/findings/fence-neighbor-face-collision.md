# Fence neighbor-face collision connections
1.11.2, Ornithe Feather: `net/minecraft/block/FenceBlock.java`, `shouldConnectTo(WorldView, BlockPos)` (124–131), `resolveVirtualProperties(BlockState, WorldView, BlockPos)` (159–163), and `addCollisions(...)` (59–80).
1.12, Ornithe Feather: `net/minecraft/block/FenceBlock.java`, `shouldConnectTo(WorldView, BlockPos, Direction)` (124–134), `resolveVirtualProperties(...)` (163–167), and `addCollisions(...)` (59–80); `net/minecraft/block/FenceGateBlock.java`, `getFaceShape(...)` (175–181); `net/minecraft/block/StairsBlock.java`, `getFaceShape(...)` (140–161).
Difference: 1.11.2 connects to a neighboring same-material fence, any fence gate, or a non-pumpkin solid-blocking cube. In 1.12, each direction supplies the neighbor's `FaceShape`; non-exception `SOLID` faces connect, while `MIDDLE_POLE` connects only for a same-material block or a fence gate. The fence gate reports `MIDDLE_POLE` only for a horizontal face whose clockwise-Y axis matches the gate facing axis; otherwise it reports `UNDEFINED`. The connection booleans feed unchanged center and arm collision boxes, including arms extending to Y=1.5.
Movement meaning: with the same fence and neighbor states, a gate whose axis is transverse to the queried neighbor face connected in 1.11.2 but not in 1.12. That changes the fence arm collision submitted to `World.getCollisions`; a local player's `Entity.move` can therefore clip against a different horizontal/upper-body obstacle. Face-shape classification of stairs also creates state/direction-specific connection cases in 1.12; its full dependency combinations remain for the pair auditor to enumerate.
