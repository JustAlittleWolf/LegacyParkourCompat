## Query route and dependency model

In 1.12, `net/minecraft/block/state/StateDefinition.java` forwards `BlockState.getCollisionShape(WorldView, BlockPos)` and `getFaceShape(WorldView, BlockPos, Direction)` to its owning `Block`, preserving state, world, position, and face arguments. `World.getCollisions` gathers block collision boxes through these state/block providers; `Entity.move` queries the swept region and clips vertical, then horizontal movement. Thus any changed box emitted by an old-era block provider can affect local-player movement. A shape provider that only affects support/placement state is a different path: it changes vanilla block state and is excluded from historical movement emulation under the project invariant to keep vanilla states.

The 1.12 `Block.getFaceShape` fallback returns `SOLID`; classes with explicit overrides replace that default. This is not interchangeable with 1.11.2's old solid/full-block predicates in every consumer. The pair coverage report’s 63-file marker screen is a locator, not proof of provider closure. The classifications below are bounded to the provider declarations and call paths named here.

## Collision providers and callbacks screened

The exact pair coverage report’s paired method-body screen searched common block sources for `addCollisions`, `getCollisionShape`, `getShape`, `isFaceSolid`, `beforeCollision`, `onEntityCollision`, `onSteppedOn`, `applyMaterialDrag`, `shouldConnectTo`, and related shape methods. It reported no other changed collision-box constants or `Box` constructor lines outside the five candidate operations. The following remaining provider groups were explicitly located and bounded:

- Non-full geometry providers `BrewingStandBlock`, `CauldronBlock`, `HopperBlock`, `MovingBlock`, `PistonBaseBlock`, `PistonHeadBlock`, `RedstoneWireBlock`, `SnowLayerBlock`, and `TripwireBlock` use `addCollisions` or `getCollisionShape`. The source screen did not identify a new box constant/branch affecting the local-player path beyond the candidate operations. `MovingBlock` delegates to its block entity (`MovingBlock.java:129–138`), and piston head/base providers are state/extension-sensitive; their full state and timing combinations remain open rather than accepted as unchanged.
- `AbstractPressurePlateBlock`, `ButtonBlock`, `CactusBlock`, `CauldronBlock`, `CobwebBlock`, `EndPortalBlock`, and `TripwireBlock` expose `onEntityCollision` callbacks. The paired method screen did not identify a changed body among these outside the bed candidate. Whether and when each callback reaches the local player is not exhaustively traced here; direct block callback/response inventory remains open in the pair report.
- `StairsBlock.onSteppedOn` forwards to its base block (`StairsBlock.java:246–247`). No changed callback body was reported outside the assigned candidates. The collision/step interaction for every virtual stair shape remains open.
- `MagmaBlock.onSteppedOn`, ordinary `Block.beforeCollision`/`onSteppedOn`, and `SlimeBlock.beforeCollision` bodies were body-screened as unchanged in the pair report. `BedBlock.beforeCollision` is excluded as the assigned bed candidate.
- `PortalBlock.onEntityCollision`, end portal interactions, liquid interactions, pressure plates, tripwire and button activation can mutate entity/world/player state. This report does not classify their complete eligibility, timing or call graph; no new accepted movement operation is inferred from a callback name or unchanged callback body alone.

A local player collision path is established for ordinary block shapes through `World.getCollisions` and `Entity.move` in the current coverage report. This report does not independently close all provider internals or callback-to-player gates for moving pistons, portal/teleport events, block activation, or client event writers. They remain explicit open dependencies for the pair author/auditor.
