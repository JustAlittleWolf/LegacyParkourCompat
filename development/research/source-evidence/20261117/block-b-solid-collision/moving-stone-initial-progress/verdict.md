## Scope

## Geometry on the old moving-block path

The old static east-facing head shape is a plate at local x `[0.75,1]` with full y/z extent and an arm at x `[0,0.75]`, y `[0.375,0.625]`, z `[0.25,0.75]` (`block/PistonHeadBlock.java:87-92,95-119,127-149`). A correctly state-shaped head at world x=1 would therefore have a plate ending at x=2; applying the old extension transform to that plate would leave it ending at x=1.75. That apparent 0.25 gap is not the shape sampled by the moving-head collision lookup.

`MovingBlock.getCollisionShape` asks `movedState.getBlock().getCollisionShape(world, pos, movedState)` and then adjusts the returned world box (`block/MovingBlock.java:118-129,162-193`). For a head moved as a block entity, this invokes the inherited `Block.getCollisionShape`, which returns the block singleton’s current `min/max` fields without calling `PistonHeadBlock.updateShape` (`block/Block.java:346-350`). Those fields start as the full unit cube in the `Block` constructor (`Block.java:198-202`); the head’s static `addCollisions` temporarily sets plate/arm bounds but resets them to the full cube before returning (`PistonHeadBlock.java:87-92`). Consequently the normal moving-head lookup at progress 0 samples `[1,2] × [0,1] × [0,1]`, subtracts 1 from east-facing `maxX`, and `Box` normalization leaves a zero-width head plane at x=1. The paired old Stone moving block at x=2 similarly becomes a zero-width plane at x=2. The old extended base’s state-shaped bound is x `[0,0.75]`, but its world `addCollisions` override resets to the full cube before collection, so its collision box is x `[0,1]` (`PistonBaseBlock.java:174-219`).

Thus the relevant old collision set is a full base through x=1 and moving-head/Stone planes at x=1 and x=2. The 0.25 gap derived from the state-shaped head plate does not describe the old moving-head collector; the actual plane-to-plane interval is 1 block wide.

## Modern Stone shape and strict collection/clipping

In 26.2, `MovingPistonBlock.getCollisionShape` delegates to its moving block entity (`world/level/block/piston/MovingPistonBlock.java:107-113`). For extending non-source Stone at progress 0, `getExtendedProgress` is `-1` and the moved state’s collision shape is translated from destination x=2 to x `[1,2]`, with full y/z extent (`PistonMovingBlockEntity.java:102-109,366-393`). `PistonBaseBlock` creates the Stone BE at the destination and creates the head as a separate source BE (`PistonBaseBlock.java:316-334`). The Stone shape is not restricted to exclude the old head-to-Stone interval.

For old player movement, `Entity.move` gathers block boxes from the player box expanded by the requested movement and then clips the single moving axis (`entity/Entity.java:439-459`). `World.getCollisions` scans cells intersecting that sweep and invokes `Block.addCollisions` (`world/World.java:891-925`); collection uses strict `Box.intersects` (`block/Block.java:339-343; util/math/Box.java:153-158`). The x=1 or x=2 plane is collected only when the swept player x interval strictly crosses it and y/z overlap strictly. An exact endpoint touch is not collected, but the requested move already ends at contact. A positive skim across a plane is collected. If collected, `Box.intersectX/Y/Z` clips at the corresponding plane only when the starting face is at or beyond it and the other two axes strictly overlap (`Box.java:93-149`).

Modern `Entity.collide` sends `boundingBox.expandTowards(movement)` through block collision collection and clips the requested axis (`world/entity/Entity.java:1141-1144,1195-1199,1244-1259`). `BlockCollisions` scans one cell beyond the swept box, and `Cursor3D` includes the maximum scan coordinate (`world/level/BlockCollisions.java:54-61; core/Cursor3D.java:20-40`). For the counterexample sweep `[1.2,1.8] × [0.99,2.8] × [0.2,0.8]`, cell `(2,0,0)` is scanned as a face-boundary cell. The `MOVING_PISTON` registration sets `dynamicShape()` (`world/level/block/Blocks.java:844-854`); dynamic shapes have no cache (`BlockBehaviour.java:511-516`), and `hasLargeCollisionShape()` treats a missing cache as large (`:577-579`), so the face-cell gate admits it (`BlockCollisions.java:89-102`). Its moving BE supplies the translated positive-volume Stone shape `[1,2] × [0,1] × [0,1]`; the Boolean-AND join with the swept AABB is nonempty. `VoxelShape.collide` clips at the matching face (`world/phys/shapes/VoxelShape.java:252-303`). An inward move from exact face contact collects after any positive skim and clips to zero; an endpoint that merely touches needs no clip.

## Bounded counterexample

Choose a 0.6-wide player with x interval `[1.2,1.8]`, z interval strictly inside `[0,1]`, and y interval `[1,2.8]`. This player is nonembedded at progress 0: it only touches the modern Stone top at y=1, and it is strictly separated from the old base `[0,1]` and both old x planes at 1 and 2. Request a small downward-only movement (`dy < 0`).

In 1.8.9, `World.getCollisions` enumerates x from `floor(minX)` to the exclusive `floor(maxX + 1)` limit (`world/World.java:891-925`); for unchanged x `[1.2,1.8]`, it visits x=1, not x=2. The moving head at x=1 contributes only the plane x=1, which does not intersect that interval; the base at x=0 is not visited. No old block collision is collected and Y motion is not clipped. In 26.2, the swept box enters the modern Stone shape at x `[1,2]`; the initial y face is exactly touched, and any positive downward skim creates a strict shape overlap. `VoxelShape.collide` resolves the downward movement to zero at y=1. This is a reachable one-axis collision-clipping difference, not an inference from the excluded direct piston-push routine.

The same gap explains the boundary conditions. If the player’s x interval is wholly inside `(1,2)`, neither old plane intersects it; the old head moving-block path does not provide the state-shaped plate. The modern Stone’s x cross-section does intersect it. At x endpoints exactly 1 or 2, the old zero-width plane itself is not intersected by mere touch; a positive axial skim across the plane is collected and clips at contact. For orthogonal motion, strict x overlap is required for either old plane, whereas modern Stone needs only strict overlap with `[1,2]`; the interval `[1.2,1.8]` satisfies modern overlap and avoids both old planes. Exact y/z boundary touch is similarly excluded until the sweep produces positive overlap, after which only modern has the Stone shape in this x interval.

**Verdict:** a bounded initial-phase, nonembedded one-axis counterexample exists for Y or Z movement. The 0.6 width does not defeat it: it fits within the actual 1-block interval between old moving-head and Stone planes. The source-state-shaped head would reduce the apparent gap to 0.25, but the old moving-block collision lookup bypasses that state-specific shape. The modern non-source Stone shape has no moving-head restriction that would preserve the old plane-only result. This does not decide direct pushing, diagonal motion, later phases, or initially embedded states.
