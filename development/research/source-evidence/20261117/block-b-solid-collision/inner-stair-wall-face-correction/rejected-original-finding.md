# Inner stair side-face result differs for wall connectivity

1.13.2, Ornithe Feather Gen2 build.2: `net/minecraft/block/StairsBlock.java`, `getFaceShape(BlockView, BlockState, BlockPos, Direction)`, lines 108–128; `net/minecraft/block/WallBlock.java`, `shouldConnectTo(BlockState, FaceShape)` lines 51–55 and `getPlacementState(BlockPlaceContext)` lines 73–92.

1.14.4 control, Ornithe Feather Gen2 build.2: `net/minecraft/block/StairsBlock.java`, `getShape(BlockState, BlockView, BlockPos, CollisionContext)` lines 99–105, shared quadrant shapes and `SHAPE_INDICES` lines 37–53; `net/minecraft/block/Block.java`, `getCollisionShape` lines 375–378 and `isFaceSolid`/`isFaceFull` lines 402–412; `net/minecraft/block/state/BlockState.java`, `m_87223014(BlockView, BlockPos, Direction)` lines 326–327; `net/minecraft/block/WallBlock.java`, `m_71853799(BlockState, boolean, Direction)` lines 48–52 and `getPlacementState(BlockPlaceContext)` lines 59–72. The 1.14.4 `WallBlock` control is within the 1.14 release line; these endpoints do not establish the exact introduction patch inside 1.14.

Difference: 1.13.2 wall connectivity asks the stair for a `FaceShape` and accepts `SOLID` (the stair itself is not an exception or middle-pole case). For horizontal faces, let F be `FACING`, CW be `F.clockwiseY()`, and CCW be `F.counterClockwiseY()`. Its results are: `STRAIGHT` = {F}; `INNER_LEFT` = {F, CW}; `INNER_RIGHT` = {F, CCW}; either outer shape = {}. In 1.14.4 the stair has no `FaceShape` override used by the wall: the wall asks whether the neighbor's collision-shape face is full. The shared stair quadrant geometry gives: `STRAIGHT` = {F}; `INNER_LEFT` = {F, CCW}; `INNER_RIGHT` = {F, CW}; either outer shape = {}. Any horizontal queried face not listed is false. Both TOP and BOTTOM halves have the same horizontal matrix: their base and step quadrants mirror vertically, while the full-width horizontal boundaries remain these same sides.

Concrete axis fixture: with `FACING=NORTH`, both halves of `STRAIGHT` connect NORTH only. Both halves of `INNER_LEFT` connect NORTH+EAST in 1.13.2, but NORTH+WEST in 1.14.4; `INNER_RIGHT` has the inverse side swap. Both outer shapes connect on no horizontal face in either endpoint. The rule covers all legal horizontal facings by replacing NORTH with F and rotating CW/CCW as defined above.

`WallBlock` queries the face opposite its neighbor offset: a wall north of a stair asks the stair's SOUTH face, east asks WEST, south asks NORTH, and west asks EAST. For a north-facing `INNER_LEFT` stair, a wall east of it therefore gets a true WEST connection bit in 1.13.2 and false in 1.14.4; a wall west of it gets the inverse EAST bit. The wall stores these bits as directional properties, and its outline and collision shapes index those properties (`WallBlock.getShape`/`getCollisionShape`, 1.13.2 lines 32–38; 1.14.4 lines 34–40). Thus this predicate changes which physical wall arm is present. This finding makes no trajectory claim.

The common oak, spruce, birch, jungle, brick, sandstone, quartz, acacia, prismarine and purpur stair registrations instantiate this shared `StairsBlock` provider on both sides; their material/base-state arguments do not alter the face rule.
