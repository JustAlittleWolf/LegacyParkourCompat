## Ordered reachable tick and state inventory

1. **Input and local tick entry — covered; core handoff unchanged.** `KeyboardInput.tick` → `Input.tick` → `LocalClientPlayerEntity.tick` → inherited player/living tick handoff is unchanged. My SHA-256 checks confirmed identical `Input.java`, `KeyboardInput.java`, `ClientPlayerEntity.java`, and `LocalClientPlayerEntity.java` on both sides. The assigned core report also checked `Minecraft` movement dispatch. The separate use-key/action path in `Minecraft.doUse()` is changed and directly affects the later local-player movement tick (item-use finding below).
2. **Player/living movement decisions — covered for identical input/state; one upstream state delta.** Paired tick, `mobTick`, jump, `moveRelative`, travel, and `Entity.updateVelocity` gates/order retain ground, water/lava, flying, sprint, ladder, friction, jump-boost and float arithmetic. `LivingEntity` retains `stepHeight = 0.6F`; ordinary travel reaches inherited `Entity.move` (1.8 `LivingEntity.java:1161/1186/1214`; 1.8.1 `:1167/1192/1220`). `PlayerEntity.java` differs only in death/respawn state copying (portal fields and enchantment seed), not ordinary movement. The client-side item-use state is not identical when the new mining gate suppresses item start; see the next subsection.
3. **Position/velocity writers and collision tick — covered, one source delta with applicability open.** Ordinary `Entity.move` preserves the initial Y→X→Z clip, collision flags, step gate, two raised route candidates, horizontal-progress comparison and final position/velocity writes. At 1.8 `Entity.java:526` the selected raised route requests `dy = -this.stepHeight`; at 1.8.1 `Entity.java:522–530` it requests `-var33` or `-var81`, the selected route's clipped ascent. This method is reachable from player travel, but a reachable ordinary-player terrain/input witness remains unproven.
4. **Second-pass query and provider closure — covered.** Both versions query collisions from the starting shape expanded horizontally by requested motion and upward by `stepHeight`. `Box.expanded` with positive Y preserves the starting `minY`; `World.getCollisions` and `Block.addCollisions` use strict shape intersection. The checked provider inventory includes the paired block collision/addition/callback providers, moving-block entity, liquid subclass, world-border fallback, and entity-shape list. Fixed-state collision geometry, slime callbacks, soul-sand velocity scaling, cobweb response and ladder/vine predicates produce no player-motion delta. Mutable `BlockPos` reuse in collector loops is consumed synchronously by the checked providers. `WorldChunk` only reorders excluded-entity comparison and `getShape().intersects(bounds)`; membership is unchanged, and the extra accessor read on the excluded entity only obtains its current box.
5. **Item-use inventory state and movement modifiers — one in-scope difference; other consumers covered.** The changed `PlayerInventory.java` comparisons replace `TestForBlockCommand.matchesNbt` with `NbtUtils.matches` in inventory counting; they do not alter movement inventory selection or equipment reads. Attribute/equipment paths and the client status-effect consumer are unchanged. Jump Boost consumption in `LivingEntity.jump()` is unchanged. Separately, 1.8 `Minecraft.doUse()` has no mining guard; 1.8.1 wraps its complete right-click path in `if (!interactionManager.isMiningBlock())` (`Minecraft.java:1169–1171`). `Minecraft.tick()` consumes attack-key clicks before use-key clicks (`:1554–1559`); `startMiningBlock` sets the flag for ordinary non-instant survival mining (`ClientPlayerInteractionManager.java:130–181`). For an item-use action in air while the flag is set, 1.8 falls through to `ClientPlayerInteractionManager.useItem`, which sends `PlayerUseC2SPacket` and calls `itemInHand.startUsing(world, player)` on the client (`:321–334`). An exact representative is `BowItem.startUsing` (`BowItem.java:85–88`), which calls local `player.setItemInUse` when the player is creative or has an arrow. `PlayerEntity.hasItemInUse()` then becomes true; unchanged `LocalClientPlayerEntity.mobTick` scales both movement inputs by `0.2F` and resets double-tap sprint (`:548–551`), and checks `!hasItemInUse()` before both sprint-start paths (`:565`, `:577`). The 1.8.1 gate prevents that local state transition while mining, so it directly changes next-tick local movement input/sprint eligibility. This is distinct from potion-effect application: `PotionItem.finishUsing` adds effects only under `!world.isClient`; that server-side producer remains out of the client movement claim.
6. **External player responses — covered for the client response path.** The changed hunks in `ClientPlayNetworkHandler.java` concern experience-orb coordinates, non-player entity interpolation, and dragon body yaw. Player correction (`handlePlayerMove`: relative position/velocity handling followed by `LocalClientPlayerEntity.updatePositionAndAngles` and movement-packet echo), entity velocity/explosion/status-effect consumers and local-player tick sources are unchanged. Thus there is no changed direct local-client player response to an identical server packet.

## Other changed-path dispositions

- `World.java` collision collector and material-contact loops reuse mutable positions and cache world-border flags. The paired block/provider reads found no retained mutable position or altered collision/drag result. `FlowingLiquidBlock`/`LiquidBlock` flow and drag behavior is unchanged; `getFlowAngle`'s `atan2` replacement is consumed by the client liquid renderer only.
- Changed block declarations/providers outside the movement collision list change map-color/constructor plumbing or non-movement neighbor survival/attachment predicates. `SnowLayerBlock` changes self-support from exactly seven layers to seven or more, while its fixed-state collision shape is unchanged. Vanilla block states remain external inputs.
- `Minecraft.doUse()`'s new mining guard is a direct player-movement delta via the local `hasItemInUse()` slowdown/sprint gates documented above; it is not excluded as mere interaction parity. Potion effect application remains a separate server-only path.
- `ThrownEntity` and `ProjectileEntity` change projectile angle math and entity-hit selection (`distanceTo` to `squaredDistanceTo`); portal handling also changes. `EnderPearlEntity.onCollision` teleports a thrower only under `!world.isClient`, and for a connected `ServerPlayerEntity` in the same world who is not sleeping. Projectile hit-scan and teleport production are therefore server-side non-player physics; the client player correction consumer for a given packet is unchanged. These changes are excluded by the logical-client-only/player-only movement invariant, not treated as a changed local-player response.
- Other reported deltas in rendering, GUI, world generation, damage/hurt direction, health/breath, player death transfer and server entity pushing are outside the fixed-profile local-player movement tick. Vehicle and other non-player physics remain out of scope.
