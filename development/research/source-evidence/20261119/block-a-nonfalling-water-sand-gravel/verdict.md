# Independent verdict — sand and gravel liquid-flow provider

## Verdict

**SOURCE CONFIRMED** for the bounded vanilla-provider claim across all 24 stable releases from 1.8 through 1.12.2. Dry sand, red-sand metadata, and gravel each use the `FallingBlock` default material `Material.SAND`; base `Material.blocksMovement()` is true. In `LiquidBlock.getFlow`, that predicate skips the dry-side `side.down()` depth read. The exact logic is continuous across the audited releases; source syntax/signatures change, but the gate and contribution formula do not.

**STABLE FIXTURE CONFIRMED, WITH A CAUSALITY LIMIT.** A supported dry sand/gravel side at `(1,64,0)` over stone is a stable, legal fixture queried by the ordinary water-flow path. Its east contribution is zero. A separate north level-1 water source beside the level-0 center source contributes nonzero north flow, so the same normal player water query produces and applies a nonzero vector. Since the sand/gravel support below is stone, reading that block would also yield liquid depth `-1`; this stable fixture proves ordinary query applicability and the stated zero contribution, but does not isolate a movement delta caused by the `blocksMovement()` gate.

**OPTIONAL 1.8 SAME-TICK LOCAL PLACEMENT CONFIRMED.** The exact 1.8 client source supports ordinary block-item placement of that stable, stone-supported side before the local player's water-collision query later in the same `Minecraft.tick()` call. This is local prediction/call-order evidence only; it establishes no packet timing guarantee or runtime parity.

## Exact source findings

- In every release, `Block.java` registers `sand` as numeric block ID 12 and `gravel` as ID 13. `Blocks.SAND` resolves the single `sand` block; `Blocks.GRAVEL` resolves `gravel`. Red sand is not a separate block/provider: 1.8 and 1.8.1 use the separate `SandBlock__Variant` enum, and 1.8.2–1.12.2 use nested `SandBlock.Variant` state (`SAND`/`RED_SAND`). `GravelBlock` has no variant.
- `SandBlock` and `GravelBlock` extend `FallingBlock`. Its no-argument constructor calls `super(Material.SAND)`; `Material.SAND` is a plain `new Material(...)`, and the base `blocksMovement()` returns true. Neither provider overrides that material behavior. `FallingBlock.canFallThrough` accepts water, so sand/gravel with water directly underneath is not a stable static fixture. Its scheduled fall is guarded by `!world.isClient`; no transient lifetime or server-update overlap is claimed.
- `LiquidBlock.getFlow` tests the horizontal neighbor's liquid depth. If dry (`< 0`), it reads the neighbor's material predicate and samples `side.down()` only when `!blocksMovement()`. If that lower sample is liquid, it contributes `(belowDepth - (centerDepth - 8))` along the direction. For ordinary same-height liquid neighbors, the formula is `(neighborDepth - centerDepth)` along the direction. The separately hashed source methods group by exact body into 1.8, 1.8.1, 1.8.2–1.8.9, 1.9–1.9.2, 1.9.3–1.12.2; these groups preserve the same dry-side gate and flow math.
- At the stable fixture, center source depth is `0`; north level-1 water contributes `Direction.NORTH * (1 - 0)`, yielding a nonzero north vector. East sand/red sand/gravel is dry and has `Material.SAND.blocksMovement() == true`, so it contributes zero without consulting its stone support. Other dry exits are enclosed. A level-1 flowing north cell with sole neighboring level-0 center source computes level 1 again and converts to a source; `LiquidSourceBlock` disables random ticks. With stone support and the enclosure unchanged, the north level-1 gradient is an ordinary stable source configuration.
- In the 1.8 ordinary entity tick, `Entity.baseTick()` calls `checkWaterCollisions()`. That queries `World.applyLiquidDrag` with the player's shrunken/down-grown bounds; the center water is in the scan while the north flow neighbor is read by `getFlow`, not directly scanned. `World.applyLiquidDrag` calls the block's `applyMaterialDrag` and, for non-flying players (`PlayerEntity.hasLiquidCollision()`), applies the normalized vector at `0.014`. This is the ordinary local player query, not a vehicle or non-player claim.

## 1.8 local placement trace

The scoped placement trace in the author's companion is source-confirmed independently: `GameRenderer.pick` refreshes the crosshair target at the start of `Minecraft.tick`; a normal use-key path calls `doUse` before the later `world.tickEntities()` call. The player's ray trace passes `false` for liquid handling, so it can target the solid face behind the center water. From `(0.5,64,0.5)`, clicking the west face of a solid block at `(2,64,0)` offsets a sand/gravel block item to `(1,64,0)`. The target is supported by stone at `(1,63,0)` and does not overlap the player box. `ClientPlayerInteractionManager.useBlock` synchronously invokes `ItemStack.useOn` after sending the packet; `BlockItem.useOn` passes the replaceable/obstruction checks and calls `world.setBlockState`, whose client path writes the local chunk without a server-only guard. `world.tickEntities()` then ticks the loaded `LocalClientPlayerEntity`, whose inherited `Entity` tick calls `checkWaterCollisions`. Thus the local side block is visible before that player's same-call water query.

This placement does **not** create water below the sand/gravel and therefore does not make the gate's below-water counterfactual observable. It closes the optional same-tick stable-placement/reachability question only.

## Coverage and boundary

Coverage is the entire 24-release roster for the material assignment, registrations, red-sand/shared-block mapping, flow gate, falling support, and ordinary liquid-query dependencies. The 1.8 placement path was source-traced and hash-checked separately. This verdict does not assert a changed velocity from the stable supported side, an indefinitely supported sand/gravel-over-water state, guaranteed client/server schedule overlap, broader materials, falling-face behavior, body-water policy, portal/piston effects, or runtime parity. It is not a pair-freeze or whole-tick-graph audit.
