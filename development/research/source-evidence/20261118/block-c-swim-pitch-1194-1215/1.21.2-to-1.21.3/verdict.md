### Frozen operation and dependencies

In both `Player.java:1445–1472`, passenger travel immediately delegates to `super.travel` and skips the Player writer. Otherwise, if `isSwimming()`, Player reads `getLookAngle().y` into a `double`; chooses `0.085` when that widened value is `< -0.2`, else `0.06`; and performs the Y nudge only when lookY `<= 0.0`, `jumping` is true, or the fluid at `BlockPos.containing(getX(), getY() + 1.0 - 0.1, getZ())` is nonempty. The ORs short-circuit in that order. The sample therefore checks the fluid state at the player's X/Z and Y + 0.9 coordinate, and accepts any nonempty fluid. When gated in, it reads current delta movement and writes `delta.add(0.0, (lookY - delta.y) * factor, 0.0)`. These are double operations after `getLookAngle().y` is widened to double; the look vector's components are float-derived.

`Entity.java:2192–2193` makes `getLookAngle()` use authoritative current `getXRot()` (pitch) and `getYRot()`. `Entity.java:1619–1627` calculates pitch radians as float `xRot * (float)(Math.PI / 180.0)`, then returns vector Y as unary-negated `Mth.sin(pitchRadians)`. `Mth.java:32–35,48–49` initializes a 65,536-element `float[]` by storing `(float)Math.sin(i * Math.PI * 2.0 / 65536.0)` for each integer index, and computes sine as `SIN[(int)(angle * 10430.378F) & 65535]`. Thus angle-times-scale is float arithmetic; Java casts that float to `int` by truncation toward zero, masks to 16 bits, indexes the float table, and the resulting float is then widened to double when Player stores `.y` in its `double` local. This LUT, index, and cast chain is byte-identical in the pair.

The selected swim state is also inherited unchanged: `Entity.isInWater()` (`Entity.java:1252–1254`) returns `wasTouchingWater`; `Entity.updateSwimming()` (`Entity.java:1285–1290`) includes sprinting, water, and nonpassenger gates; Player's override (`Player.java:1476–1482`) clears swimming while flying and otherwise delegates. This is context for the `isSwimming()` gate, not a second finding.

After the Player nudge, its ordinary nonflying branch delegates to `LivingEntity.travel`. For locally controlled movement, `LivingEntity.java:2178–2188` first samples `level().getFluidState(blockPosition())`, then selects fluid travel only if `(isInWater() || isInLava()) && isAffectedByFluids() && !canStandOnFluid(sample)`; otherwise it selects fall-flying or air travel. `isAffectedByFluids()` is true and `canStandOnFluid(FluidState)` is false here (`LivingEntity.java:550–552,2164–2166`). In water travel (`LivingEntity.java:2216–2243`), after water slowdown and relative input, it calls `move(MoverType.SELF, getDeltaMovement())`, then reads the resulting delta, applies horizontal-collision/climbable handling, multiplies by slowdown and `0.8F`, and writes fluid-fall-adjusted movement. Both versions use the same branch, sample order, and values.

The call resolves to inherited `Entity.move(MoverType, Vec3)` (`Entity.java:619–693`), whose body hash is equal in the pair. Its ordinary physics path applies edge backoff, collision resolution, position and collision-state updates, fall handling, block callbacks / movement emission under their gates, and block-speed-factor scaling of horizontal delta. The no-physics branch directly adds the requested vector to position. No Player or LivingEntity move override intervenes in the cited path.
