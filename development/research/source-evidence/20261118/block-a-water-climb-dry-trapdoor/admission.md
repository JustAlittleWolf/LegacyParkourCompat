## Tick and fluid-scan order

In all three releases, `Entity.tick` calls `baseTick`, which invokes the water-state refresh (`Entity.java:332–394`). The refresh calls `checkWaterState`, which asks `m_69693160(FluidTags.WATER)` and stores its boolean in `inWater` (`Entity.java:947–976`; 1.13.2 source lines 961–976). `LivingEntity.tick` calls `super.tick()` before its AI/travel work (`LivingEntity.java:1689–1692`), then its travel tick dispatches `moveRelative`; normal player overrides delegate to `super.moveRelative` outside their flying branch (`LivingEntity.java:1907` in 1.13, `:1926` in 1.13.1/1.13.2; `PlayerEntity.java:1451/1457`, `:1458/1464`, `:1458/1464`).

The shared scan contracts the standing entity box by `0.001`, computes integer bounds with floor(min) and ceil(max), and checks `world.isAreaLoaded(...)` before enumerating cells (`Entity.java:2507` in 1.13, `:2509` in 1.13.1/1.13.2). It loops each cell in those bounds and accepts a tagged fluid when `cellY + fluidState.getHeight() >= contractedBox.minY`; it does not require the fluid cell to be the player’s center/feet cell. If the area is unloaded, it returns false before reading fluid states. The local witness therefore assumes ordinary loaded chunks.

`FluidState.getHeight()` delegates to `Fluid.getHeight`; `FlowingFluid.getHeight` is `state.getLevel() / 9.0F`, and a source water state has level 8 (`FlowingFluid.java:448–450`, `WaterFluid.java:136–145`). Java evaluates `8 / 9.0F` as binary32 `0.8888888955116272`; in `double e = q + fluidState.getHeight()`, integer `q` is promoted to float, so at `q=64` the sum rounds in binary32 to `64.88888549804688F` and is then widened to double. This exceeds the contracted feet value `64.001`. The scan sets `inWater=true` before the travel branch reads it. All three `moveRelative` bodies gate water travel on `isInWater()` unless the player is flying (`LivingEntity.java:1467`, `:1486`, `:1486`). Their water branch moves first and retains the optional `collidingHorizontally && canMove(... + 0.6F ...)` correction (`:1594/1633`, `:1613/1652`, `:1613/1652`); it does not require water to occupy the feet cell.

## Constructive same-tick state

Use the following block coordinates and standing player position (coordinates are illustrative exact vanilla geometry):

- Player position `(x=0.9, y=64.0, z=0.32)`, standing size `0.6F × 1.8F` (`PlayerEntity.java:448–451`, also pose sizing `:334–359`). `Entity.setPosition` computes `float halfWidth = width / 2.0F`, which is widened to double for the box (`Entity.java:309–315`); `halfWidth` is `0.30000001192092896`. The actual, uncontracted movement box is `[0.599999988079071,1.200000011920929] × [64,65.8] × [0.01999998807907105,0.620000011920929]`. Feet-floor sampling uses `floor(x)=0`, `floor(box.minY)=64`, `floor(z)=0`.
- Cell `(0,64,0)`: open, unwaterlogged trapdoor facing north. Its open collision shape is the north 3/16 slab (`TrapdoorBlock.java:30–35,45–60`). The player’s actual max Z `0.620000011920929` is short of the slab’s min Z `0.8125`, so the body has no trapdoor collision overlap. `isClimbing` reads this feet cell and accepts the open trapdoor when the ladder below has the same facing (`LivingEntity.java:1046–1068` in 1.13.2; corresponding method in each release).
- Cell `(0,63,0)`: north-facing ladder. Its 3/16 collision shape ends at Y=64, touching but not volumetrically overlapping the player box whose minY is 64 (`LadderBlock.java:22–45`). The predicate’s state/facing check is satisfied.
- Cell `(0,63,1)`: solid backing block for ladder placement. `LadderBlock.canBePlaced` checks the solid south face for a north-facing ladder (`LadderBlock.java:53–63`). The backing block ends at Y=64 and only touches the player box’s minY, so it is outside the player AABB.
- Cell `(1,64,0)`: source water. It is outside the trapdoor state. For the fluid scan only, contracting the actual player box by `0.001` gives X `[0.600999988079071,1.199000011920929]`, Y `[64.001,65.799]`, Z `[0.02099998807907105,0.619000011920929]`. Thus the scan includes `(1,64,0)`; its float-calculated surface `64.88888549804688` is above the contracted minY `64.001`, so the scan admits water. The uncontracted movement box also overlaps the non-solid fluid cell; this does not create a block-collision obstacle.
- A separate full block at `(0,65,-1)` can obstruct a small negative-Z input. The **uncontracted** movement box begins at Z=`0.01999998807907105`, leaving that positive gap to the block’s Z-max `0`; a negative-Z displacement of `0.03` crosses the gap, clips against the block, and sets horizontal collision. The player center then remains near Z=`0.32` (at the clipped boundary it is about `0.30000001192092896`), so `floor(z)` remains 0. The block has positive X/Y overlap with the player and is distinct from the water cell. No contracted-fluid-scan bounds are used for this collision calculation.

These states coexist in one vanilla tick. The unwaterlogged trapdoor and ladder need not contain water; the player body scan can admit water from an adjacent fluid state. Water may be a stable source in that adjacent cell. A flowing-water trajectory depends on upstream sources and fluid updates and is not established here. The witness does not require water to propagate through the trapdoor or an already-running player to move through the obstruction for multiple ticks.

## Clearance consequence and limits

The old `0.3F` correction is optional and occurs only after the water branch’s move/damping. Its `canMove` delegates to `doesNotCollide`, which requires both no solid collision and `!world.containsFluid(candidateBox)` (`Entity.java:465–471`). `WorldView.containsFluid` checks every block cell intersected by the candidate AABB and returns true for any nonempty fluid state (`WorldView.java:213–235`). In the witness, the actual uncontracted candidate box raised by the `+0.6` clearance amount still intersects water cell `(1,64,0)`, so the clearance admission fails even without a ceiling; a low ceiling at Y=66 independently rejects that candidate while leaving the standing box clear. The water-travel branch itself is entered before this optional clearance check.

The admission conclusion holds independently in 1.13, 1.13.1, and 1.13.2: the fluid scan, feet-cell climb rule, player dimensions, and travel gate support the same witness. It is a source-reachable same-tick state, not runtime proof that an arbitrary approach trajectory lands on or maintains it. Exact long-term water propagation and player trajectory remain outside this marginal admission check.
