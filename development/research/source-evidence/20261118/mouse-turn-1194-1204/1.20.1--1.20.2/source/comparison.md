## Bounded trace

1. **Captured callback and local accumulator.** `net/minecraft/client/MouseHandler.java`: 1.20.1 `onMove` lines 175–210 and `turnPlayer` 212–254; 1.20.2 `onMove` 187–222 and `turnPlayer` 224–266. With a normal captured window, active window, player present, unscoped first-person state, and smooth camera off, `onMove` adds `(callbackX - xpos)` / `(callbackY - ypos)` to double accumulators, calls `turnPlayer`, then stores the new cursor coordinates. `turnPlayer` computes `sensitivity = option * 0.6F + 0.2F`, its cube, and that value times `8.0`; after resetting smooth-camera state, the unscoped branch multiplies accumulated X/Y by the latter factor. It zeros both accumulators, chooses integer Y sign from invert-Y, calls the tutorial hook, then calls the local player's `turn(x, y * sign)` when present. The initial ignored move seeds cursor coordinates before accumulating, so the witness uses a subsequent finite callback delta. Those exact two method bodies compare equal across the pair. Device callback registration, queueing, and operating-system delivery are excluded.

2. **Virtual pitch setter and clamp.** `net/minecraft/world/entity/Entity.java`: 1.20.1 `turn` lines 395–407 and `setXRot` 3273–3281; 1.20.2 `turn` 397–409 and `setXRot` 3303–3309. `turn` casts each double input to float, multiplies by `0.15F`, applies pitch then yaw via setters, clamps pitch to `[-90.0F, 90.0F]`, updates old pitch/yaw by the same float deltas, clamps old pitch, then notifies a vehicle if present. `setXRot` itself rejects non-finite float input through `Util.logAndPauseIfInIde` and otherwise stores it; the clamp is in `turn`, after that virtual setter call. Source search finds no player/LivingEntity override of `setXRot` in either tree, so this local player's call reaches the inherited `Entity.setXRot`. The `turn` and setter bodies compare equal across the pair.

3. **Reached player move, with flight-mode gates.** `net/minecraft/world/entity/LivingEntity.java` and `net/minecraft/world/entity/player/Player.java`: `LivingEntity.aiStep` calls `updateFallFlying`, constructs the movement input vector and, when not taking the ridden path, calls `travel(input)` (1.20.1 lines 2562–2572; 1.20.2 lines 2591–2601). This reaches `Player.travel` (1.20.1 lines 1446–1473; 1.20.2 lines 1450–1476). Its unchanged `abilities.flying && !isPassenger()` branch calls `super.travel`, then scales pre-move vertical velocity by `0.6`, resets fall distance and clears the fall-flying shared flag; the else branch also calls `super.travel`.

   For the reachable Elytra mode, `Player.tryToStartFallFlying` accepts an airborne, non-fall-flying, non-water player without Levitation when the chest Elytra is enabled (1.20.1 lines 1576–1586; 1.20.2 lines 1580–1590). `LivingEntity.updateFallFlying` maintains the shared flag only when it is set, the player is airborne and not a passenger or Levitation-affected, and the chest item is an enabled Elytra (1.20.1 lines 2610–2634; 1.20.2 lines 2639–2664). `LivingEntity.travel` selects its fall-flying branch when `isFallFlying()` after its water/lava branches (1.20.1 lines 2068–2107; 1.20.2 lines 2102–2141). That branch reads both `getXRot()` and `getLookAngle()` and uses them in the glide acceleration, then updates velocity and calls `move(SELF, deltaMovement)`. Thus an ordinary finite mouse turn can reach a real player movement calculation whose math consumes the turned pitch/look state. The corresponding `Player.travel`, `LivingEntity.travel`, and Elytra activation/gate method bodies compare equal across the pair.
