## Call path and exact order

- In 1.14 Feather, `LocalClientPlayerEntity.move` calls `autoJump` after `super.move` (`LocalClientPlayerEntity.java:846–852`). With AutoJump enabled, `ticksToNextAutojump <= 0`, grounded, non-sneaking and not riding, the method builds a horizontal probe and calls `this.world.getCollisions(this, box, Collections.emptySet())` (`:858–860`, `:901–915`). It iterates the flattened bounding boxes and **breaks at the first box intersecting either side ray** (`:918–945`). That box supplies `s`; after height checks the timer is set to `1` only for rise `u` with `0.5 < u <= 1.2` (`:948–952`).
- The receiver is the inherited `Entity.world`, whose declared type is `World` (`Entity.java:113`), initialized by the local-player constructor from its `ClientWorld` argument (`LocalClientPlayerEntity.java:116–124`; `ClientWorld.java:65`). `World` implements `WorldAccess` (`World.java:64`), which extends both `EntityView` and `WorldView` (`WorldAccess.java:21`). Thus the three-argument overload resolves to `WorldView.getCollisions`; its `getEntityCollisions` virtual call resolves to `WorldAccess.getEntityCollisions`, which delegates to `EntityView` (`WorldAccess.java:74–75`). The latter obtains intersecting entities and yields each entity's collision box before blocks (`EntityView.java:45–59`); the client world inherits `World.getEntities` (`World.java:875–886`).
- At 1.14 `WorldView.getCollisions` sets `stream` to entity collisions for an ordinary entity away from the border (`WorldView.java:131–144`). The return is `Stream.concat(stream, Stream.concat(stream4, aggregatedFullBlockShape))` (`:146–194`): entities precede individually admitted block shapes and the aggregated full-cube shape. This is the order consumed by the AutoJump iterator above.
- The exact older comparator is not a `WorldView`-only simplification: 1.13.2 AutoJump calls `this.world.getCollisions(this, box)` (`LocalClientPlayerEntity.java:935`). `WorldView`'s two-argument overload delegates to the shape-based overload (`WorldView.java:166–168`); the runtime `World` override calls `WorldAccess.super.getCollisions` first and appends `getEntityCollisions` (`World.java:1755–1757`), so blocks precede entities for this caller.
- The exact newer comparator, 1.14.1 Yarn AutoJump calls `this.world.getCollisionShapes(this, lv13, Collections.emptySet())` (`ClientPlayerEntity.java:900–903`). Its runtime world is `ClientWorld extends World` (`ClientWorld.java:63`) and `World implements IWorld` (`World.java:64`). `ViewableWorld.getCollisionShapes(entity, BoundingBox, exclusions)` concatenates the block/border spliterator first and `this.getCollisionShapes(entity, shape, exclusions)` second (`ViewableWorld.java:129–194`); `IWorld` delegates that entity stream to `EntityView` (`IWorld.java:72–75`). Thus this neighboring endpoint is blocks-first as well.

## Bounded 1.14 witness (source-derived, no launch)

Use a normal player width/height (`0.6 × 1.8`; `EntityType.java:399–401`), no jump boost, no border overlap, and a horizontal forward approach while grounded. All positions below use world coordinates:

- Standing player before the tick: center `(1.2, 1, 0)`, feet on a solid floor at `y=1`; player AABB `x=[0.9,1.5]`, `y=[1,2.8]`, `z=[-0.3,0.3]`.
- One unconnected fence at block `(1,1,0)`, no same-height horizontal fence/gate/solid neighbors. Pane/fence shape construction (`PaneBlock.java:31–45, 85–88`; `FenceBlock.java:24–30`) gives the collision post `x=[1.375,1.625]`, `y=[1,2.5]`, `z=[0.375,0.625]`.
- One unmounted boat centered at `(0.6,1,1.2)`. Its registered dimensions are `1.375 × 0.5625` (`EntityType.java:146–148`), and `BoatEntity.getCollisionShape` returns its entity AABB (`BoatEntity.java:113–117`; `Entity.java:304–311`): `x=[-0.0875,1.2875]`, `y=[1,1.5625]`, `z=[0.5125,1.8875]`.

The prior standing AABB has a positive gap from the fence post in Z (`0.075`) and from the boat in Z (`0.2125`); boat and fence are disjoint in X by `0.0875`. The fixture therefore starts collision-free apart from support contact with the floor. A forward input into the fence is blocked at the fence face. With `getSpeed()=0.1` (base player attribute in `PlayerEntity.java:172`; AutoJump uses `f=this.getSpeed()` and `p=max(f*7,1/l)` at `LocalClientPlayerEntity.java:866, 898–903`), its predictive side rays extend past both boxes. For a forward ray, the two X tracks are center `1.2 ± 0.3`, i.e. `0.9` and `1.5`: the boat intersects the `0.9` track and the fence intersects the `1.5` track. The broad probe also admits both boxes. The upward block checks above either candidate center are air in this layout.

Consequently, in 1.14 the entity-prefix yields the boat first: `s=1.5625`, so relative rise from player feet is `0.5625`, which satisfies `0.5 < u <= 1.2`; the loop breaks and sets `ticksToNextAutojump=1`. If the same candidates are ordered blocks-first, the fence post is the first hit: `s=2.5`, `u=1.5 > 1.2`, so the timer is not set. This demonstrates a reachable source-level first-hit/timer distinction from an ordinary standing approach. It is not a trajectory parity verdict for an executed game.
