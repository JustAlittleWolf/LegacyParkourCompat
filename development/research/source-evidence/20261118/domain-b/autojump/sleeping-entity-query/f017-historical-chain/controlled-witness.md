# F017 AutoJump controlled historical witness: 1.14.2 → 1.14.3

## Result

**COMPLETED — the audited gates compose into one conditional, source-supported witness.** Under the stated vanilla setup, the sleeping player can reach the same AutoJump detector in both releases. Its only relevant old/new difference in this witness is the final entity-collision query: the boat is excluded in 1.14.2 and admitted by the 1.14.3 `1.0E-7` expansion. That changes the detector timer from zero to one. A later, source-permitted wake interval can let that pending timer produce an ordinary grounded jump without a physical jump-key press.

This is an exact-source composition, not runtime validation. The wake packet interval is possible but asynchronous; source does not guarantee that a particular run receives it between the detector callback and the next local player tick.

## One chronological construction

1. **Prepare the world and input.** Use a remote multiplayer world, a permission-level-2 operator, an AutoJump-enabled client with its enabled/cached state active, and a flat patch of full-cube floor with two clear air blocks above it. Leave the original bed elsewhere in place for now. Use no Jump Boost, fluids, nearby colliding entities, or obstructions. Keep physical jump input false. Set a narrow GUI (for example, 320×240 scaled pixels), open the inventory and its recipe book, and bind `key.forward` to an otherwise-unused mouse button. Press it on an unhandled area outside the recipe-book controls and hold it through the target movement tick. The audited route leaves `Input.movementForward` positive while sleeping; it does not claim sleeping travel moves the entity. The mouse event is necessary: the ordinary keyboard route is consumed by the screen.

2. **Enter sleep while preserving the screen.** Sleep normally in the bed. The client bed activation does not predict sleep; server tracking supplies the local player's sleeping pose and sleeping position. The server tracker has a self-send path for the local `ServerPlayerEntity`, and the client applies those tracked values to its local entity. Since the inventory was already current, the sleeping-chat auto-open branch requiring `currentScreen == null` does not replace it. The inventory is non-pausing, so the remote client continues ticking and samples the held bound mouse input.

3. **Relocate the sleeper onto support.** Have the operator run `/spreadplayers` on the sleeping player over the prepared flat patch. This route requires permission level 2, selects entities without a sleeping exclusion, and uses the entity teleport path without the wake call used by `/teleport`. It preserves the tracked sleeping position and pose. The pile places the player centered in a destination cell at the support top. Let the next local movement resolve against that support: the accepted head-space witness reaches the post-collision AutoJump callback with `onGround` true, while `floor(bbox.maxY)` and the block above are both air. Keep the original bed intact until after the detector has scheduled its timer.

4. **Place the stationary boat relative to the actual pile.** Because `/spreadplayers` chooses a pile, measure the resulting player center `(x + 0.5, floorY, z + 0.5)` and use the permitted operator `/summon` route to place an oak boat at `(x + 1.5, floorY + (double)0.2F + 4e-8, z + 0.5)`, with `NoGravity:1b` and zero velocity. Keep the floor clear and put air at the block above the boat's horizontal center. The exact offset gives a positive vertical gap of about `4.0e-8` from the sleeping box top: too large for the old strict final intersection, but smaller than the new `1.0E-7` expansion. The boat does not overlap the player's box or either head-space cell. Its bounding-box top yields a rise of about `0.7625F`, inside the detector's `> 0.5F && <= 1.2F` range. With zero realized horizontal movement, the positive forward input and eastward yaw select the already-audited fallback ray probes that intersect the boat. The source-supported no-gravity, zero-velocity boat remains stationary.

5. **Reach the version-dependent detector result.** At the next grounded local movement callback, the two head-space block checks pass and the input/cooldown/ground/sneak/vehicle gates are stipulated to pass. The detector's ray query reaches entity collision shapes. In 1.14.2, the entity candidate search has a `0.25` envelope but its final strict intersection uses the original query, so the positive gap excludes the boat and this detector leaves its timer at zero. In 1.14.3, the final query itself expands by `1.0E-7`; the boat intersects it, the sampled block above its center is air, and the accepted rise writes `field_3934 = 1`. This is the paired F017 outcome; the AutoJump detector method itself is otherwise the same for the relevant path.

6. **Wake after scheduling, before consuming the timer.** Now destroy the original bed and allow the server's sleeping-bed validation to wake the player. With the old bed gone, the wake routine skips the bed-side reposition branch, clears sleeping metadata, and changes to standing pose. The server sends the wake animation and then a self position-look packet for the already-relocated coordinates; client handling can restore that destination and preserve the grounded state. Arrange for those packets to be processed after step 5's callback and before the next local player tick. On that next tick, the positive AutoJump timer sets `input.jumping = true` after raw input sampling. If the player is awake, still grounded, has zero jump cooldown, and has clear jump space, normal jump handling can produce the physical jump without a physical jump-key press. The audited wake source permits this packet window, but does not guarantee its timing in every run.

## Coexistence check and boundaries

The gates share a compatible state: sleep metadata and the pre-existing pass-events inventory can coexist; mouse-bound forward input remains in the raw `Input` vector even though sleeping movement fields are cleared; spread-player relocation can retain sleep and land the player on support; the empty head-space cells are directly above the player while the boat sits ahead and just above the box; the boat affects only the raised AutoJump entity-query probes; and the old bed can remain intact through scheduling, then be destroyed for wake. The chosen boat's lower edge is separated from the player box, so it adds no entity contact that would disturb the support or sleep path.

The witness is conditional on operator command execution, the stated server/client setup, enabled AutoJump and other detector predicates, the specified terrain/boat placement, and the wake packet window. It establishes neither reliable runtime timing nor complete tick-level parity. No product applicability, implementation, intermediate 1.14 patch boundary, test, build, or runtime result is claimed.
