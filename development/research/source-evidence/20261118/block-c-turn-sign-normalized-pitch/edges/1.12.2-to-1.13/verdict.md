## Difference and reachable witness

In 1.12.2, camera input is float-valued and the setter assigns `pitch = (float)(pitch - input * 0.15)` before clamping; its caller scales integer mouse deltas using float intermediates. In 1.13 the cursor delta and sensitivity product are double-valued, and the setter assigns `pitch = (float)(pitch + cursorDeltaY * 0.15)` before clamping. The input signs are reversed by the old/new coordinate paths, so normalize by the intended pitch change: an intended negative pitch increment uses a positive old setter argument (subtracted) and a negative new `cursorDeltaY` (added). The final `pitch` is the value consumed by the next movement tick.

A reachable caller witness uses the same float mouse-sensitivity setting `0.08160785585641861` (`0x3da72205`), with smooth camera and invert-mouse disabled, and one unit of vertical mouse motion: old `Mouse.y = +1`, new accumulated GLFW `dy = -1`, sign-normalized to negative pitch change. The old float sensitivity path yields magnitude `q12 = 0.12345351278781891`; the new double path yields `q13 = 0.12345351225261912`, with `float(q13) == q12`. This is exactly the setter-width comparison: the normalized intended magnitude is retained as double by the new setter and narrowed to float on the old path. From the legal initial float pitch `0.007531699258834124` (`0x3bf6cc79`), the old setter produces `-0.010986328125` (`0xbc340000`) and the new setter `-0.010986327193677425` (`0xbc33ffff`). Both are within the ordinary `[-90,90]` clamp.

For the next tick, take a local player with `isFallFlying` flag 7 true, an intact Elytra in the chest slot, `onGround=false`, no vehicle, no water/lava, no Slow Falling, open air, yaw 0, and initial velocity `(0.2, -0.1, 0.3)`. The `flyingTick` gate preserves active gliding for this state; the local player then reaches `moveRelative`’s dry Elytra branch. Both versions use the same 65,536-entry sine-table construction and the same `sin`/`cos` index expression. With their respective pitch floats, `i = pitch * (float)(π/180)` is `-0.0001917476038215682` (old) versus `-0.00019174757471773773` (new). The common lookup expression indexes entries 65534 versus 65535, producing `sin(i) = -0.0001917476038215682` versus `-0.0000958738019107841`. Thus the difference survives the LUT; it is not an artifact of changing the table.

Numerically evaluating the two exact flight expressions with all other state held fixed, the gliding term `horizontalSpeed * -sin(i) * 0.04` is `0.000002765423270104076` versus `0.000001382711635052038`. After the source’s vertical and horizontal updates and drag, velocity is `(0.1782, -0.10583132763262496, 0.3136844936349598)` versus `(0.1782, -0.10583566381631249, 0.3136857256310266)`. The pitch-only difference is therefore `+4.336183687531081e-6` in Y velocity and `-1.231996066775043e-6` in Z velocity (old-result minus new-result) before collision movement.

This witness also isolates the pitch effect from the changed flight equation: at this state `m`/`n` is exactly `1.0`, and without Slow Falling the 1.12.2 vertical base term `-0.08 + m * 0.06` and 1.13 term `0.08 * (-1 + n * 0.75)` both equal `-0.02`. Re-evaluating either release’s flight body once with each pitch gives the same pitch-only velocity delta above. The shared trig table likewise has identical initialization and lookup procedures. This closes the proposed LUT/flight-law dependency for this witness.

No tests, runtime, game launch, implementation, product, Git, network, wiki, or source generation were used.
## Phase-two independent comparison

The frozen source report at `c-turn-precision-1122-113-motion-source/report.md` was rehashed before this comparison and matched the authorized SHA-256 `bbe505676fe8eaf0df3096ffbe15fc21c0996985afbcea345fc1ebe8b6e71999`. Its exact sign-normalized setter witness independently checks out: with upward pitch intent `δ = 2.343714356422422`, 1.12.2 receives `-float(δ) = -2.3437142372131348F` and subtracts it, while 1.13 receives `+δ` as double and adds it. Starting at `0.0F`, the results are adjacent floats `0.35155713558197021F` (`0x3eb3ff4c`) and `0.3515571653842926F` (`0x3eb3ff4d`). The signs, casts, setter formula, and binary32 outputs agree with the exact `Entity.updateLocalPlayerCamera` declarations and bodies.

The caller widths also agree with source: 1.12.2 `GameRenderer` uses integer `Mouse.y`, a float sensitivity gain, and calls the float setter; 1.13 `MouseHandler` accumulates cursor deltas as doubles, computes a double gain, and calls the double setter. This confirms the ordinary call paths support the respective widths. The frozen report's matched `δ` is a normalized pre-conversion setter intent, not a claim that identical raw mouse samples or event scheduling yield identical values after the two callers' distinct scaling operations. I do not infer such a device-level equivalence.

The next-tick movement witness is independent of device scheduling in the scoped sense: once the two explicit current-pitch values are present, `LivingEntity.moveRelative` reads `pitch` directly in the active, dry, nonpassenger Elytra branch; this branch uses neither prior-pitch interpolation nor the input event queue. The report evaluates each version's own movement formula with the fixed state and isolates the pitch-only difference within each version. The finding remains conditional on the setter having installed its pitch before that movement tick; the source calculation does not claim a particular hardware/tick schedule or a runtime observation. Its cos-lookup bucket crossing, legal Elytra gate, and reported displacement deltas are internally consistent with the cited source expressions.
