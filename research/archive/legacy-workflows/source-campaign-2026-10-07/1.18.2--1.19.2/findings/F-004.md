## Source-level difference

Sculk Sensor blocks exist in both endpoints. A's `Entity.move()` skips all `stepOn()` callbacks when `isSteppingCarefully()` is true, and A's SculkSensorBlock inherits the no-op base callback. B removes the careful-step guard from the grounded `stepOn()` dispatch and adds a SculkSensorBlock override that activates an inactive sensor for any non-Warden entity on the server. That callback does not check `isSteppingCarefully()`.

## Reachability and dependencies

Local player grounded movement -> `Entity.move()` resolves the support block and, when grounded, calls `stepOn()` -> B's SculkSensorBlock callback checks server side, inactive phase and non-Warden entity -> sensor enters ACTIVE phase with power 15 and a `STEP` vibration frequency. Under those same conditions, A does not invoke the callback for a careful step and its SculkSensorBlock has no direct step activation. Resource tags, listener response, redstone surroundings and later movement consequences are not established here.

## Consequence and uncertainty

Source-proven: on a grounded careful step over an inactive Sculk Sensor, B invokes a player-facing block callback that can change the sensor to ACTIVE and power 15; A skips the callback. The callback does not directly write player position or velocity. Predicted consequence: the changed sensor/redstone state can alter later world interactions. No world layout, redstone response or trajectory was simulated. The separate `GameEvent.STEP` event-dispatch change is F-003 and may interact with listener behavior, but is not needed for this direct callback path.
