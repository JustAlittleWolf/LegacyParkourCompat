## Source-level difference

For an unsuppressed downward LocalPlayer collision on an existing slime block where `0 < -currentMovement.y < getEffectiveGravity()`, A dispatches to the slime callback, which changes the negative Y velocity to its positive inverse for a living entity. B's restitution threshold selects zero and the vertical restitution formula writes Y velocity zero. The low-speed threshold itself guarantees this B result; it does not depend on the initial entity restitution value.

The B threshold is the exact method value `getEffectiveGravity()`, not an assumed constant. The precondition excludes bounce suppression (shift held) and a suppression-tagged block; the cited slime registration and tag resource establish that ordinary slime is not tag-suppressed.

## Reachability and dependencies

In both versions `LocalPlayer` extends `AbstractClientPlayer`, which extends `Player`; `Player` extends `Avatar`, and `Avatar` extends `LivingEntity`. The paired `canSimulateMovement()` override in `Player.java` returns `!this.level().isClientSide() || this.isLocalPlayer()`, so the local client player enters the guarded movement response. Normal player travel delegates through `LivingEntity`, whose movement path calls `Entity.move`; the broader player tick/travel call graph remains tracked in the open run slices.

Bounce suppression reads the player's shift state in B `Entity.isSuppressingBounce()`. The movement result is the player entity's Y velocity, consumed by subsequent player travel/ticks. This finding concerns client-side source behavior for the local player; it makes no claim about server synchronization or damage.

## Consequence and uncertainty

The source proves that this bounded low-speed landing writes positive Y velocity in A and zero Y velocity in B under the stated conditions. No trajectory or gameplay reproduction was run. The source pair establishes only that the change is present between these endpoints; its first release is unknown within the interval.
