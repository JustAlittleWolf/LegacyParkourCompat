- Packet and interpolation dependency: `handleEntityPositionSync()` sets the position-codec base, then for a non-local-authoritative entity calls `moveOrInterpolateTo()` when it is ticking and squared distance is at most 4096, otherwise it calls `snapTo()`. For a `LivingEntity`, `getInterpolation()` returns its handler; the identical A/B `moveOrInterpolateTo()` delegates to `InterpolationHandler#interpolateTo()`, whose identical method sets active steps to the configured length (default 3). `isInterpolating()` is identical and tests `hasActiveInterpolation()`, which is identical and returns `steps > 0`. Therefore on the active-interpolation branch A's positive guard repositions the local Player while B's negated guard skips it; on the snap/no-active branch the result is reversed. The separate B change inside `InterpolationHandler#interpolate()` affects the non-player entity's collision-probe AABB construction; it does not change the just-established `steps > 0` guard value before this handler returns.
- Exact precondition and vanilla packet path: the client receives `ClientboundEntityPositionSyncPacket` for a non-local-authoritative entity that has the local Player as an indirect passenger. A vanilla example is a Player who remains aboard a pig after no longer holding a Carrot-on-a-Stick: the pig's controlling-passenger predicate becomes false while its passenger relation remains, and `ServerEntity` sends the position-sync packet after `teleportDelay > 400`. If the client is ticking the pig and the packet displacement is at most 64 blocks, both versions establish active interpolation before evaluating the reversed guard, yielding the different direct Player position call. The rider method sets the Player position and resets its old position/rotation snapshot.
