## Source-level difference

If the local player is a passenger, presses shift, is not flying or swimming, can fit in the crouching pose, is not visually crawling, and has nonzero movement input, A reports slow movement to `Input.tick`. B forces the crouching flag false and reports no slow movement. With a sneak-speed factor below `1.0`, A scales horizontal input impulses and B leaves them unscaled. Both then copy those impulses into the local player movement vector and run the ordinary player travel path.

## Reachability and dependencies

The local call chain is `LocalPlayer.aiStep()` -> `Input.tick()` -> `LocalPlayer.serverAiStep()` -> `LivingEntity.aiStep()` -> `LivingEntity.travel()` -> `moveRelative()`/`Entity.move()`. The slow-movement factor includes the sneaking-speed enchantment bonus; that effect is a Stage 6 dependency. This finding concerns the player's local movement path while mounted. Any additional effect on the vehicle's movement input is outside the finding and the separate non-player movement scope.

## Consequence and uncertainty

Source-proven: A scales the local player's horizontal movement input under the stated passenger/shift conditions, while B does not. This changes the movement vector passed to player travel when the factor is below one. Later mounted-position synchronization and the resulting trajectory were not runtime-tested.
